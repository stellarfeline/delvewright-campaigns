VIEW 1 — THE WHOLE CASTLE FROM THE NORTH APPROACH.

ONE single full-frame view. Not a grid, not panels, not insets. No text, no labels, no interface.

A player stands on the grassy north approach road about 45 blocks north of the castle, at eye height, looking south at the castle's north front. The whole castle fills the middle of the frame, with sky above it and wooded hills beyond.

THE CASTLE — Doune Castle, built in blocks at one and a half blocks to the metre — seen from the north:
- On the LEFT, the east end: the GATEHOUSE, the Lord's Tower. The tallest and heaviest mass: a sheer rectangular tower block about 27 blocks wide and 40 blocks tall, flat-faced, with a crenellated parapet flush with its walls and a steep gabled garret roof rising inside the parapet. At its north-east corner a stepped round tower rises to the same height. At the bottom centre of its north face, one tall arched gate opening with a raised iron portcullis showing in the arch; small arrow loops beside it; a few small square windows high up; a projecting machicolation box under one upper window, right over the gate.
- In the MIDDLE, running west from the gatehouse: the GREAT HALL RANGE. A lower long block about 30 blocks long, its north wall plain and high with small windows, its steep dark pitched roof ridge running east-west and sitting LOWER than the gatehouse parapet.
- On the RIGHT, the west end: the KITCHEN TOWER. A second tall rectangular tower about 25 blocks long, lower than the gatehouse, crenellated parapet, gabled garret roof, a small stair turret on one corner, a big chimney stack.
- Glimpsed behind the ranges on the left and right: the top of the 16-block curtain wall that closes the courtyard, crenellated, with small open corner rounds.

THE APPROACH, in the foreground: a straight gravel road runs from the viewer to the gate. It crosses THREE parallel dry ditches, each with a grassy bank rampart between them; the road crosses the ditches on a timber bridge of dark oak planks and log posts. Grass, a few flowers, a stone boundary marker by the road.

BEYOND: the castle stands in a green valley; behind it, a rim of wooded hills with spruce and oak trees against the sky.

This picture is how the castle is recognised: a dominant tall gatehouse on the left, a long lower hall, a second tower on the right. Majestic, solid, lived-in, not a ruin.
