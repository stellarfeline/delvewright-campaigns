## 0. Choice: Doune Castle (near Stirling, late 14th c.)

- **Bodiam is rejected on two counts.** Its interior "is ruinous" [CAS-B], and its rooms survive "mainly as footings" [GH-B], so any reconstruction is conjecture. Its moat alone measures "155m by 115m" [GH-B], which breaks the footprint budget.
- **Doune's rooms stand and are roofed.** They were re-roofed in the 1880s [WP-D].
- **Doune is compact.** Gatehouse 18 x 13 m, hall 20 x 8 m, kitchen tower 17 x 8 m [WP-D]. It is defended by rivers and ditches, not a lake [WP-D].
- **Doune's adjacency chain is explicit.** Lord's hall → door → great hall; hall → serving hatches → kitchen [WP-D].
- **Castell Coch:** interior "purely High Victorian" [WP-CC].
- **Burg Eltz:** a shared-family castle with 100+ rooms in eight towers of 30–40 m [WP-E]. It has no single-household logic.
- **Caernarfon, Harlech, Vincennes, Bran, Kilkenny:** not researched. Judged too large or too remodelled, from memory, not verified.

## 2. Functional plan and adjacency

**Layout.** The entrance is on the north, through the gatehouse at the NE. The great hall runs west from it, with its dais at the east end against the lord's hall [US]. The kitchen tower closes the west end [WP-D]. The S and E sides are bare curtain wall: a fourth range was planned but never built [WP-D], [BE].

**Gatehouse**
- **Ground:** the passage lies behind a portcullis and an iron yett and is "pierced by arrow loops and murder holes" [BE]. Guardrooms with gunloops [WP-D]; a porter's room and a prison [LF]. There is no ground-floor stair up [WP-D].
- **NE round tower basement:** a well [WP-D].
- **1st floor, Lord's hall:** reached by an external stair from the courtyard. It is vaulted, with a double fireplace and a carved oak screen [WP-D], [GOB]. The musicians' gallery dates from the 1880s [WP-D]. A machicolation under its north window covers the gate [WP-D]. An original door leads to the great hall [WP-D].
- **2nd floor, Duchess' hall:** has a 2.6 m fireplace [US]. An oratory in the south wall holds a "piscina and credence" [WP-D], [EC].
- **Round tower:** the duke's and duchess's bedchambers, each with a fireplace; a sleeping closet and a latrine [LF].
- **Upper floors:** chambers, with mural passages out to the wall-walk [WP-D].

**Great hall**
- External stair from the courtyard [WP-D].
- Central hearth with a louvre and no fireplace [WP-D], [LF].
- Large windows [WP-D]; a dais at the east end [US].
- Three barrel-vaulted cellars below [WP-D], [EC].

**Servery**
- A triangular anteroom with "two large serving hatches with elliptical arches" [WP-D], [LF].
- A screened passage to the kitchens [US]; "the buttery connects to the kitchen" [EC].

**Kitchen tower**
- Vaulted kitchen at hall level: the 5.5 m fireplace, a bread oven and drains [WP-D], [LF].
- A cellar below [WP-D].
- Guest rooms above, including the "Royal Apartments": "two bedrooms plus an audience chamber" [WP-D].
- A stair turret added in 1581 [WP-D].

**Courtyard and curtain**
- Central well and three external stairs; kiln or oven footings on the south wall [WP-D].
- Round turrets at the corners, bartizans at the mid-points [WP-D], [GOB].
- A west postern under a machicolated turret, and a continuous wall-walk [WP-D].

**The circulation chain:** kitchen → hatches → hall screens end → hall → dais → door → lord's hall → stair → duchess' hall and oratory → bedchambers. The occupants sort cleanly:
- Guests live above the kitchen.
- The garrison holds the gate and wall-walk.
- The cellars serve the hall.

## 3. Tour route

1. **North approach.** Three ditches and a rampart guard the one level side [WP-D]. The castle stands where the Ardoch Burn joins the Teith, 8 miles NW of Stirling [WP-D], "gateway to the Highlands" [CSP].
2. **Gate passage.** Robert Stewart, Earl of Menteith from 1361 and later Duke of Albany, built Doune; a charter was sealed here in 1381 [WP-D]. Attackers met a portcullis, a yett, then 14 m of murder holes [BE]. Albany governed Scotland [GOB].
3. **Courtyard.** Four ranges were planned and three built [BE]; the 18 m well secured water [WP-D]. In 1746 Jacobite-held prisoners escaped down knotted bedsheets, John Home among them [WP-D].
4. **Lord's hall.** The duke's own hall sits above the gate he controlled [GOB]. The gatehouse could hold out alone even if the rest fell [BE]. The gallery and panelling date from the 1883 restoration [WP-D], [US].
5. **Duchess' hall and oratory.** A private upper hall with an altar alcove [WP-D], [EC]. After Murdoch's execution in 1425 Doune became royal [WP-D]. It then served as a hunting lodge and as the dower house of three queens [GOB].
6. **Great hall.** "One of the best preserved great halls in Scotland" [BE], with smoke rising through its louvre [LF]. Montrose occupied the castle in 1645 [WP-D].
7. **Servery and kitchen.** Food passed out through the hatches [WP-D]. The fireplace was "large enough to roast entire animals" [LF].
8. **Royal apartments.** A suite for royal guests [WP-D]; Mary, Queen of Scots stayed at Doune [GOB].
9. **Wall-walk.** Scott called it "gloomy yet picturesque" [WP-D]. Monty Python (1974), *Game of Thrones* (Winterfell) and *Outlander* filmed here [WP-D]. The castle passed to state care in 1984 [WP-D].

## 4. Period furnishing

- **Great hall:**
  - High table on the dais; trestle side tables [WP-GH].
  - A screens passage with "a timber screen with two openings", and a minstrels' gallery above [WP-GH].
  - A central hearth venting "through a vent in the roof" [WP-GH].
  - Scottish halls also held "the cupboard and silver plate; the hanging chandelier… ornamental weapons… cloth and napery" [WP-GH].
- **Lord's hall:** a wall fireplace with a carved overmantel; an oak screen [WP-GH], [GOB].
- **Kitchen:**
  - Spits sized from "quails to whole oxen", with dripping basins [WP-MC].
  - Cranes with "adjustable hooks"; cauldrons, mortars and tripods [WP-MC].
  - A bread oven and drains [LF].
- **Servery:** serving hatches; buttery [WP-D], [EC].
- **Oratory:** an altar; a piscina "near the altar… for washing the communion vessels"; a credence niche [WP-P], [EC].
- **Bedchambers:** a fireplace, a sleeping closet, a latrine [LF].
- **Garderobe:** "a simple hole discharging to the outside" [WP-G].
- **Gate:** portcullis, yett, murder holes, arrow loops and gunloops [BE], [WP-D].

## 5. Exterior character

- **Stone:** coursed sandstone rubble, dressed quoins, lighter Ballengeich stone for the dressings [WP-D]. It reads as dark stone [EC].
- **Roofline:**
  - Both towers end in a flush parapet with a gabled garret inside it [GOB].
  - The hall's pitched roof sits below the wall-walk [WP-D].
  - Open corner rounds and corbelled bartizans on the curtain [GOB].
  - A machicolated turret over the postern [WP-D].
- **Windows:** large in the hall [WP-D]; round-headed and square in the upper gate tower [EC]; loops at gate level [WP-D].
- **Silhouette:** the ~29 m gatehouse block with its round NE tower dominates. A lower hall range links it to the kitchen tower, and a 12 m curtain closes the pentagon [WP-D], [GOB].
