# Doune Castle: A Guided Tour — design record

The authoritative design. Every later round is judged against it.

## What the delve is

A showcase delve: one castle, built to be looked at and walked through. There is
no combat, no puzzle and no fail state. A guide leads the party through nine
stops; at each stop, talking to the guide gives a short account of the room and
of the castle's history, and when the conversation closes the guide walks to the
next stop and waits there. Every door is open from the first minute, so a player
may run ahead, wander, or stay with the guide. The tour ends on the wall-walk.

## The model: Doune Castle

The castle is modelled on **Doune Castle** (Stirling, Scotland; built for Robert
Stewart, Duke of Albany, late fourteenth century). It is chosen because its rooms
still stand roofed and documented, its plan is a textbook working household, and
its geometry is rectilinear. Research notes and sources: `design/research.md`.

**The functional plan is Doune's own, not invented.** The household's movement
decides where every room is:

- the **gatehouse** (the Lord's Tower) holds the gate and the lord's private
  apartments above it, so the man who ruled the castle slept over the door he
  controlled;
- the **great hall** runs west from the gatehouse at first-floor level over three
  vaulted cellars; its high table stands on a dais at the east end, against a
  door into the lord's hall;
- the **servery** at the hall's west (screens) end passes food through two
  serving hatches from the **kitchen tower**, which closes the west end of the
  range, with guest lodgings — the Royal Apartments — stacked above the kitchen;
- the **curtain wall** closes the south and east sides of the courtyard, with a
  continuous wall-walk, open rounds at the corners and a postern on the west;
- the **courtyard** carries the well and the three external stairs that are the
  only ways up into the ranges: one to the lord's hall, one to the great hall's
  screens end, one to the kitchen tower.

## Orientation and scale

World north is the castle's north. The gatehouse is the north-east block; the
entrance faces north onto the approach. **Scale is 1.5 blocks to the metre** for
plan dimensions, so a room reads at its weight in-game and leaves space for
furniture; storey heights follow the same factor, capped where a room would read
empty.

| element | real | in-game (blocks) |
|---|---|---|
| gatehouse footprint | 18 × 13 m | 27 × 20 |
| gatehouse height to parapet | ~29 m | ~40 |
| great hall interior | 20 × 8 m, 12 m to ridge | 30 × 12, 16 to ridge |
| kitchen tower footprint | 17 × 8 m | 25 × 12 |
| curtain wall | 12 m high, 2 m thick | 16 high, 3 thick |
| enclosure (estimate, see research gap) | ~50 × 40 m | ~75 × 60 |
| whole piece, with approach and ditches | — | ~100 × 110 on the ground, ~50 tall |

The enclosure size is an estimate from the buildings: no reachable source gives
it. It is a design number, recorded as one.

## Exterior

Dark coursed stone with lighter dressed quoins and window surrounds. Both towers
end in a flush crenellated parapet with a steep gabled garret roof inside it; the
great hall's pitched roof sits below the wall-walk line; open corner rounds and
corbelled bartizans on the curtain; a machicolated turret over the west postern.
Large traceried windows in the hall's courtyard wall; small loops at gate level.
Round elements (the gatehouse's north-east tower, the corner rounds) are built as
stepped many-sided towers — the grammar has no smooth curve.

The north approach crosses three ditches with a rampart between them on a timber
bridge. The whole castle stands inside a `valley` surround: grass floor, a rim of
wooded hills — the Highland edge the castle guards.

## The hour

`day` + `clear`, for the whole delve. Every concept image is drawn under it.

## The tour — nine stops

Each stop is a place in the castle and an anchor the piece declares. The guide
stands at the stop; the party gathers; the talk is one conversation, two or three
screens, closing on an option that sends the guide on.

| # | stop | where | what the guide tells |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | North Approach | outside, on the approach beyond the ditches, the whole castle in view | where it stands (the meeting of two rivers, gateway to the Highlands); who built it and why; the three ditches |
| 2 | Gate Passage | ground floor of the gatehouse | portcullis, yett, murder holes; the charter sealed here in 1381; Albany governing Scotland |
| 3 | Courtyard | by the well | four ranges planned, three built; the 18 m well; the 1746 prisoners and the knotted bedsheets |
| 4 | Lord's Hall | gatehouse first floor, up the external stair | the duke's hall over the gate he held; the double fireplace; the 1883 restoration of gallery and panelling |
| 5 | Duchess's Hall | gatehouse second floor, with the oratory and a bedchamber off it | the private upper hall; the altar alcove and piscina; 1425, Murdoch's execution, and Doune becoming a royal dower house |
| 6 | Great Hall | first floor of the range, dais end | the high table and the central hearth under its louvre; one of the best preserved halls in Scotland; Montrose in 1645 |
| 7 | Kitchen | kitchen tower, through the servery | the serving hatches; the 5.5 m fireplace; the bread oven |
| 8 | Royal Apartments | above the kitchen, up the stair turret | the guest suite; the queens and Mary, Queen of Scots |
| 9 | Wall-walk | the curtain's wall-walk, south side | the view; the castle in film; state care since 1984; the fourth range that was never built |

**Places a player finds on their own**, furnished and unguided: the guardroom and
the pit prison beside the gate passage; the three vaulted cellars under the great
hall; the duke's bedchamber in the round tower; the kitchen's larder.

## Rooms and their furnishing (period, as reconstructed)

- **Gate passage**: vaulted, portcullis raised in its slot, an iron yett folded
  back, murder holes in the vault; guardroom with benches, a weapon rack, a
  brazier; the pit prison a hatch in the floor with the drop visible.
- **Courtyard**: cobbles and turf, the well with its winding frame, the three
  external stairs, a lean-to oven footing on the south wall.
- **Lord's hall**: vaulted, stone-flagged; double fireplace with a carved
  overmantel; oak screen at one end with a gallery over it; a long table, a
  great chair, a sideboard with plate, banners.
- **Duchess's hall**: fireplace, hangings, window seats, a smaller table; the
  oratory an altar niche with a piscina and a credence shelf; the bedchamber a
  curtained bed, a chest, a fireplace, a garderobe closet.
- **Great hall**: timber roof on trusses, louvre over a central open hearth;
  raised dais with the high table and canopy; trestle tables and benches down
  the length; a screen with two openings at the west end; a cupboard of plate,
  a hanging chandelier.
- **Servery**: the two serving hatches, a counter, stacked vessels.
- **Kitchen**: vaulted; the great fireplace with spits and a cauldron on a
  crane; the bread oven; work tables, barrels, hanging game; a drain in the
  floor; the larder behind.
- **Royal apartments**: an audience chamber with a canopied chair and a
  fireplace; two bedchambers.
- **Cellars**: barrel vaults, casks, sacks, a stair up into the hall.
- **Wall-walk**: parapet with crenels, the corner rounds, the bartizans.

## Guide mechanics

- One NPC, the guide. Right-clicking opens the stop's conversation; the closing
  option sets the stop's flag; the stop's quest completes and its completion
  sends the guide walking (`move-npc`) to the next stop's anchor.
- The next stop's objective carries a title and a landmark hint, so a player who
  lost the guide can find them.
- After the last stop the guide stays on the wall-walk with a farewell bark pool.
- No stop is gated. Nothing a player does can strand the guide or the party.

## Cast

- **The guide** — the only NPC.

## Classes

One class, **Visitor**: a guidebook and a spyglass for the wall-walk. No weapons.

## What the piece owes (after the design gate)

The castle is one grammar-built piece, placed as the campaign's one area. It does
not exist yet and is built after the design gate, before quests and dialogue. It
owes: the anchors of the nine stops (the first carrying the `entry` role), the
free-exploration rooms above, every room reachable on foot from the approach, its
own light, and `shown_faces` on all four sides.

## Branches and endings

None. One route, one ending.
