# The Drowned Bell — cast

Five speaking characters. All dialogue is authored here as written lines with
written player options; nothing is generated at play time. Lines below are the
English source of record — terse, spoken, no exposition dumps. Node ids are
stable.

Convention: `>` is a player option. A node lists its unlock condition when it
has one.

---

## Emeric Tallow, the Lampman

**Where:** Z0 shelf → moves to the Z2 gate passage after S1 → the Z4 chapel step
after S3. He never goes higher.
**Is:** living, sixty-three. Fifty-one years ago he was the novice who ran the
prior's order up to the belfry and rang the two tolls himself, because he was
told to and because he was twelve.
**Wants:** the bell rung, by anyone but him. He has lit the shore lamps every
night since, for people who are already dead, and he knows it.
**Changes:** he follows the player up the rock, one zone at a time, each time
further than he has been in fifty-one years. He gives the delve's tutorial in
Z0, his confession in Z4, and in END-THREE he is the only living person on the
causeway who is not running.

### E-1 · Z0, first meeting
> "You came up with this tide, then. You'll be wanting the road."
>
> "Lamp's for the flat, not for you. Anything that comes up out there goes
> landward and doesn't stop. Neither should you."

- `> What are the lamps for?` → **E-1a**: "Marking the line. Three iron stakes,
  see them? Inside is walking. Outside is fifty years of people who thought
  otherwise."
- `> What is the bell?` → **E-1b**: "One toll, a body's coming across. Two, the
  way's shut. Three, the way's open, come on. That's the whole of it. Every
  child on this coast knows it and not one of them will say it out loud."
- `> Come with me.` → **E-1c**: "No."

### E-2 · Z2, after S1
> "I have not been under this roof since I was twelve years old." *(pause)*
> "It's smaller."

- `> Who shut the gate?` → **E-2a**: "The gate was never shut. That's the thing
  nobody gets right. The causeway was dry, the gate was up, and every one of
  them turned round anyway. Because they heard two."
- `> Who rang two?` → **E-2b** *(unlocked by OPT-A, the ledger)*: "It's in the
  book, isn't it. Margin, in the prior's own hand. *Runner: Tallow, novice.*"
  *(long pause)* "I ran it up. I pulled it twice. I was twelve, and I have been
  sixty-three for a very long time."
- `> Nothing.` → close.

### E-3 · Z4, after S3 — the confession
Available regardless of OPT-A; OPT-A only lets the player already know.
> "I'll come this far. Not the tower." *(sets the lamp on the step)*
> "Take it up if you like. It's had fifty-one years of practice at being no use
> to anybody."

- `> You were the runner.` → **E-3a**: "I was. And I have thought about it every
  night since, and I'll tell you what I decided." *(pause)* "It doesn't matter
  that I was twelve. Somebody had to be the hands. He chose a number and I was
  the hands."
- `> He gave the order.` → **E-3b**: "He did. Go and see him. He's still in the
  chair. He'll tell you he saved the rock, and the awful thing is he'll be
  telling the truth as he understands it."
- `> Ring it yourself.` → **E-3c**: "I have climbed that stair once in my life
  and I rang the wrong number. I don't get to be the one who fixes it. That's
  not humility. That's the fee."

### E-4 · endings
- **END-THREE**: "Go on, then. All of you. Go on." — he stays on the shore with
  the lamp out for the first time in fifty-one years.
- **END-TWO**: "Two." *(quietly)* "Well. Now there's two of us."
- **END-SILENT**: "That'll do. That'll do as well as anything." — he does not
  relight the lamp.

## Sister Ide of the Hours

**Where:** Z4, walking the cloister round.
**Is:** drowned, lucid. The priory's hour-keeper. She was not on the flat that
night — she was inside, ringing her hand-bell for an hour that had already been
lied about, and she has been ringing it ever since.
**Wants:** the rite done correctly. Not the bell struck — the bell **rung**: at
the ebb, by a hand that owes nothing. She is the only character who cares about
the difference between an act and its form, and she is right to.
**Changes:** if the rite is completed (OPT-B) she stops walking, sits on the
plinth, and is the first down the causeway in END-THREE. If it is not, she is
still walking when the delve ends.

### I-1 · first meeting
*(she does not stop walking)*
> "Ninth hour. There is nobody to tell, and it is still the ninth hour."

- `> Is that the bell?` → **I-1a**: "This is a hand-bell and it is cracked and
  it carries eleven feet. The bell is above us and it carries eleven miles.
  Please do not confuse them; a great many people did, once."
- `> They're dead. All of them.` → **I-1b**: "Yes. The hour is not for them.
  The hour is for the hour."

### I-2 · the rite (OPT-B start)
> "You are going up to it. I can tell — you have the walk. They all have the
> walk." *(she stops)* "Then you should be taught, because striking a bell and
> ringing one are different trades and only one of them is believed."

- `> Teach me.` → **I-2a**: "Three things. It is rung **at the ebb**, or it is
  a noise. It is rung to a **count**, and the count is the message, and there
  are only three counts. And it is rung by a **hand that owes nothing** —
  because the bell answers the last hand, and a hand with a debt in it rings
  the debt."
- `> What do I owe?` → **I-2b**: "I don't know. Look in your fist. Everyone who
  came up this tide came up holding something."

### I-3 · the rite complete (OPT-B end)
> "There. Nine, ten, eleven." *(she sets the hand-bell down)*
> "That's the round finished. Fifty-one years, and it turns out it does end."

- `> Come up with me.` → **I-3a**: "No. I'll be at the bottom. Somebody sensible
  should be at the bottom when it goes."
- `> What count should I ring?` → **I-3b**: "I taught you the trade, not the
  message. The message is yours. That is not me being wise at you — it is the
  bell's own sentence, and it has been on the lip of it since before either of
  us."

### I-4 · endings
- **END-THREE**: she takes the causeway first, and the answered follow her the
  way they used to follow the hour.
- **END-TWO**: "Two." *(she picks the hand-bell back up)* "Then it is the ninth
  hour again."
- **END-SILENT**: "Ah." *(a long silence)* "Well. Now nothing is on the hour."

## Hask

**Where:** Z6, a dry side vault above the waterline.
**Is:** living, about forty. A scavenger who rowed out nine years ago to strip
lead off the roofs and misjudged a tide. He has been in the cistern since, and
he is neither brave nor sorry.
**Wants:** off the rock. He cannot cross the flat because the answered walk it,
and they walk it every low water.
**Changes:** nothing about him changes. He is the delve's control group — the
one person on Halgrave with no stake in the bell's meaning and a total stake in
its timing. That is his point.

### H-1 · first meeting
> "Don't. Whatever you're about to do with that — don't, I've made a home."
> *(beat)* "You're one of the walking ones. You're a *polite* one."

- `> What are you doing down here?` → **H-1a**: "Living, mostly. Nine years.
  Lead off the chapel roof, that was the plan. There's a lot of lead. There's
  no boat."
- `> Why not walk out?` → **H-1b**: "Because your friends walk out. Every low
  water, up the causeway, in the dark, and they don't stop for a man. I've
  tried it twice and I've got the second one's tooth marks."

### H-2 · the ask (OPT-D)
> "You're going up the tower. Course you are." *(he gets up)* "Then do me one
> thing and I'll show you the dry way round to the well."

- `> Name it.` → **H-2a**: "Ring three, and ring it **at the ebb**. Not the
  flood. Three at the ebb and the causeway's dry for two hours and I run, and I
  never come back, and I don't want the lead."
- `> What if I ring two?` → **H-2b**: "Then I stay in the hole and you've cost
  me nothing I had. I'd rather you didn't. I'd rather quite a lot of things."
- `> What if I break it?` → **H-2c**: "Break it and they never get called and
  they never stop walking, and I stay in the hole for good." *(pause)* "You
  asked. I answered. Don't take that as a vote — I've been wrong about the tide
  before."

### H-3 · endings
- **END-THREE at the ebb**: he is on the causeway ahead of everyone, running
  flat out, and he makes the mainland and does not look back.
- **END-TWO**: he is at the cistern grate when the answered come back down, and
  the delve does not follow him in.
- **END-SILENT**: "Right." *(he sits back down)* "Right."

## Prior Ancel Wray

**Where:** Z5, the chair in the far chamber. He has not left it.
**Is:** dead and completely lucid, which is worse than a ghost. He is never a
fight in any branch and never becomes hostile — the delve must not offer the
player the relief of killing him.
**Wants:** the bell never rung again. He believes — sincerely, and with an
argument the player cannot easily beat — that two tolls saved the rock from a
plague-crowd it could not hold, feed, or bury, and that three would have killed
everyone on it instead of everyone on the flat.
**Changes:** he does not. That is the point of him; the tapestries, the ledger
and the confession are the only things that move, and they only move for the
player.

### A-1 · first meeting
> "Sit if you like. Nobody has, in some time."
> *(he does not look at the player)*
> "You are going up to it. They all go up to it. In fifty-one years not one of
> them has come back down and said a word to me about it, which I confess I find
> discourteous."

- `> You rang two.` → **A-1a**: "I ordered two. Yes. Say the rest of it — say
  what three would have been. Three is *come across*, and four hundred sick
  people come across, onto a rock with one well and eleven weeks of grain, and
  no way off it, and then this rock is what the coast is. I have had fifty-one
  years to find the better answer. I would be glad of it. I have not found it."
- `> They believed the bell.` → **A-1b**: "They did. That is what a bell is for
  and that is what I spent." *(pause)* "I am not going to tell you it was worth
  it. I am going to tell you it was arithmetic, and that I did it, and that I
  did not hand it to anyone else."
- `> You handed it to a twelve-year-old.` *(unlocked by OPT-A or E-3)* →
  **A-1c**: *(the first time he looks up)* "...Tallow." *(long pause)* "Is he
  alive?" — and whatever the player answers, he says: "Then he has had the
  worse fifty-one years, and I would have said that was impossible."

### A-2 · the two facts (CP-14, always available)
> "You will want the tongue. It went down the cistern well with the man who cast
> it. The stair is broken at the first flight — Ridd did that, and he was
> thorough, and you have presumably met him."

- `> Why tell me?` → **A-2a**: "Because you cannot do it. Nobody has. And
  because if you can, I would like to know before I am finished, and I am very
  nearly finished."

### A-3 · the confession (OPT-A / G3)
The letter is in his chamber, written the week after and never sent, addressed to
the mainland parish. It is four sentences and it does not apologise; it lists
the count of the flat, by name, in his own hand, and the last line is *I have
not been able to make this number smaller.*

- `> I read the letter.` → **A-3a**: "Then you have read the only honest thing
  on this rock." *(pause)* "It is not a confession. It is an invoice. I have
  never known who to send it to."

### A-4 · endings
- **END-THREE**: "Three." *(he stands, for the first time in the delve)* "Well.
  Then I was only ever early." — and he walks down and out with them, at the
  back, alone.
- **END-TWO**: "Two. Yes." *(he does not stand)* "You will want the chair
  eventually. I recommend it. It is the only thing up here that was ever
  comfortable."
- **END-SILENT**: "Ah — no. No, that is worse than mine. Mine was a number. You
  have made it so there are no numbers." *(and he says nothing else, ever)*

## Odo Ferrier — the First Answered

**Where:** Z7, the stairhead below the belfry. Final boss.
**Is:** drowned, and further gone than Ide or the player — he can speak, in
short pieces, and the pieces are all from the same night. He rowed the dead out
of Halgrave for thirty years and rowed the living off the flat for four hours,
four at a time, until the boat went over with a full load.
**Wants:** to be the last hand. The bell answers the last hand; he means the
hand to be one that was out there. He does not want to kill anyone and says so
between the swings.
**Changes:** he is the only character whose resolution the player chooses.
Beaten, he sits down on the stair and lets the player past. Shown his own tally
(OPT-C), he stands aside without a fight and comes up behind them.

### O-1 · the stairhead, on sight
> "No. Not you. **Me.**"
> "I have made this climb thirty-seven thousand times and every one of them
> ended here, and it is going to be me."

*(the fight begins; the following are barks, not options)*
- "Four at a time. That was all she'd take."
- "Turn round, they said. They *rang* it."
- "Get behind me. GET BEHIND ME." *(this line is why he is not a monster)*

### O-2 · beaten
*(he sits down on the stair; he does not die and cannot be finished)*
> "...Then go up."
> "Ring it properly. If you can only do one thing right tonight, do the number
> right."

### O-3 · the tally (OPT-C)
*(offered instead of the fight; presenting the tally is the interaction)*
> "That's my book." *(he takes it)* "That's my book, that's my hand, that's —"
> *(he finds the last line)*
> "...I never crossed you off."
>
> "Then you're still a fare. Aren't you." *(he steps off the stair)*
> "**Passenger first.** Go on up. I'll come behind you and I'll not touch a
> thing."

### O-4 · the belfry, in every ending
He is at the player's shoulder when the bell is rung, whether he was beaten or
stood aside, and he says one line per ending:
- **END-THREE**: "That's the one. That's the one they were waiting on." — and
  he goes down the stair ahead of everyone, at a run, for the first time.
- **END-TWO**: "...Two." *(a very long pause)* "Aye. That's the number this
  place knows."
- **END-SILENT**: *(as the bell goes through the floor)* "Wait — wait — I had
  it, I nearly —" — and then nothing, for the rest of the descent.

## The unspeaking

Named presences with no dialogue, listed so later stages do not invent voices
for them:

- **Housecarl Ridd** (Z5, boss 1) — one line on sight, none after: *"He said
  nobody goes up. Nobody has gone up."*
- **the Founder** (Z6, boss 2) — no words at all. He is the only character who
  has nothing to say, because his one statement was made with a hammer.
- **the Gatewright**, **the Two Sextons**, **the Choir** — no speech. The Choir
  makes the sound of a bell and is the only place in the delve, before the
  belfry, where a bell is heard.
