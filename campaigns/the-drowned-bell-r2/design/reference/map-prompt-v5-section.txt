Draw a vertical CUTAWAY SECTION through the rock: the rock is sliced from top to bottom along its south-north axis and the near half is taken away, so the viewer looks straight into the cut. The cut face of the rock and the cut walls of every building are shown as solid sectioned mass. South is on the LEFT of the frame, north on the RIGHT. This is a section drawing, not a view of a hillside: the interiors are open to the viewer.

SUBJECT — Halgrave: a fortified priory on a tidal rock off a drowned northern coast. One place, drawn as one object.

A low oval crag of dark grey rock standing out of a wide flat of wet grey tidal sand. Its long axis runs south-west to north-east. The rock STEPS UP from south to north, and each step carries one part of the citadel, stacked ABOVE the step before it. This is a stepped mass, not a row of buildings on one level. From the bottom up, with heights in metres against the sea:

- THE FLAT, at -1.2: wide open wet grey tidal sand, drystone burial cairns heaped on it, a line of rusted iron tide-stakes standing in it, sea fog closing it in the distance.
- THE CLIFF ROAD, at +4: one narrow cut ledge climbing north across the sheer west sea-cliff, a single thin line of worked rock on an otherwise unbroken dark face, with two dark cave mouths on it.
- THE GATEHOUSE, at +2.5: a squat, wide, pale stone block built into the rock's south foot at the head of the causeway, one arched opening in its south front, flat walkable roof, and a broken sea-torn hole in its outer wall where the cliff road ends.
- THE DROWNED WARD, floor at -1.5: a wide roofless outer ward behind and BELOW the gate, FULL OF STANDING SEA WATER, ringed by a broken toothed curtain wall, its east sea-wall breached open to the sea, two ruined stone arcades standing in the water, a raised stone causeway crossing it just above the water at +0.4, sunk boats off the causeway, and a small square water-gate tower with a peaked dark metal roof and shuttered windows standing in the water at its far side.
- THE CLOISTER, at +9: a roofless cloister on the shelf above the ward, arcade ranges on four sides, their arch-heads open to the sky, grass in the paving joints.
- THE HALL AND KEEP, at +12: the one intact roofed building on the rock, long axis east-west, steep dark pitched roof, slit windows. The only solid dark shape below the tower.
- THE UPPER WARD AND RAMP, at +14: cobbles between collapsed low buildings on the crown, with a black collapse hole in the paving where a cistern vault below has fallen in.
- THE BELL TOWER: plain, square, pale, unbuttressed, standing clear of everything and set back on the crown, its foot at +14 and its belfry floor at +30 — twice the height of anything else on the rock. Its top storey is pierced by a large square opening on every face that the sky shows through, and an enormous dark bell of oxidised copper in a heavy timber frame is BUILT into that opening and nearly fills it. The belfry is a room walked around the bell, not a shelf the bell sits on.

The rock's whole west and south-west face is sheer dark unclimbable cliff falling to surf and rock teeth. The sea is ONE flat plane at height 0.0 everywhere — around the rock, over the sand, and inside the drowned ward. Nothing holds a level of its own.

LIGHT AND HAND: flat cold overcast North Sea daylight, sea fog, no sun, no cast shadows, no warm colour anywhere. Muted grey and grey-green, desaturated painted concept art. Massing and silhouette carry the recognition; no tracery, no fine ornament, no figures, no birds.

THIS VIEW SPECIFICALLY — the section exists to prove that these are separate LEVELS stacked above one another, and that one of them is underground:

Draw ONE flat sea level as a single straight horizontal line running right across the whole frame, through the rock and out both sides. Nothing holds a level of its own; every wet space in the picture is at that one line.

From left (south, low) to right (north, high), at these heights against that sea line:

- 1.2 metres BELOW the line: the tidal sand of the flat, running off the left edge of the frame, with cairns standing on it;
- 4 metres ABOVE the line: the cut cliff road, drawn beyond the cut on the far side of the rock, a narrow ledge on a sheer face;
- 2.5 metres ABOVE the line: the gatehouse, cut open to show a vaulted passage running through it with a channel down the middle of its floor, a portcullis at its south end, and a second floor above the passage;
- 1.5 metres BELOW the line: the drowned ward, a wide sunken open basin immediately behind the gatehouse whose floor is four metres below the gate's own sill, filled with sea water to the line, its raised causeway just above the water, and its far wall broken open to the sea;
- 9 metres ABOVE the line: the roofless cloister on a shelf of rock, open to the sky;
- 12 metres ABOVE the line: the hall and keep, cut open to show one tall roofed room under timber trusses;
- 14 metres ABOVE the line: the cobbled upper ward, with a hole broken through its paving;
- 30 metres ABOVE the line: the belfry floor at the top of the tall plain square tower, with the great bell built into the open belfry above it, and a long straight stair climbing the whole height of the tower inside, broken away at its first flight low down;
- and UNDER the upper half of the rock, entirely below the ground, a long brick barrel-vaulted cistern: its floor just at the sea line, its ranked brick piers and vaults marching away, the collapse shaft rising from its roof to the hole in the upper ward's paving above it with daylight falling down the shaft, and at its north end a well shaft going down to a silt bed 2.3 metres below the line.

The cistern must be clearly BENEATH the cloister, the hall and the upper ward, with solid sectioned rock between them — not a room beside them.

The sky above the rock is flat cold overcast. Sea fog closes the left and right edges of the frame.
