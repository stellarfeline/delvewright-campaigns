# Halgrave — the whole-map brief

The eight zones each have an approved reference image and were authored against
it. The **whole** had none. This is the whole's description, fixed in prose
before any tool ran, on the same terms `prefab-procedure.md` §1 fixes a scene
before geometry: what the place is, seen from outside, as one object.

It is derived from `../story.md`, `../beats.md`, `../quests.md`, `../tide.md`,
the eight images in `../concept/`, and what `../GENERATION.md` records the
produced pieces actually are. It adjudicates nothing. Where a fact forced a
judgement, the fact is written down and the judgement is left out.

Heights are metres against the **standing tide**, `0.0`, the datum `tide.md`
fixes. The reference views and every elevation below are drawn at that level.

---

## The place, in one paragraph

Halgrave is a low oval crag standing out of a wide flat of grey tidal sand, an
hour's walk off a drowned coast, with a priory on it that is also a fort. The
rock steps up from south to north in five stages and each stage is a different
zone: the sand at the bottom, a squat gatehouse built into the rock's south foot
at the head of the causeway, a wide roofless outer ward behind it that is full of
standing sea, a roofless cloister on the shelf above that, the one intact roofed
building — a long hall and its keep — above that again, and at the crown, set
back and standing clear of everything, a plain square bell tower. The rock's
whole western face is sheer dark cliff falling to surf, with one cut road-ledge
scratched across it. Under the upper half of the site, invisible from every
outside view, a brick cistern is cut into the rock; the only evidence of it above
ground is a collapse hole in the paving of the upper ward and an iron grille in
the ward's rock face. The recognition, from the shore and at the size a
silhouette survives at, is **one pale square tower with a hole of sky at its top,
standing on a stepped grey rock, with a dark band of drowned ruin around its foot
and wet sand around that.** The hole of sky is the open belfry, and the black
shape filling it is Mercy.

## The rock

An oval crag, long axis south-west to north-east, joined to the mainland only by
a causeway of wet sand running south off the site, uncovered twice a day.

- **West and south-west**: sheer cliff to open sea. This is the seaward face and
  it is undercut and unclimbable except by the one cut ledge. Surf and rock teeth
  at the bottom of it.
- **South**: the causeway head, the tidal flat, and the rock's only gate.
- **East**: the low lobe of the rock. The priory walled a ward across it when the
  sea was lower; the sea-wall on that side is breached and the ward is open to
  the sea through it. This is why the ward holds sea rather than a pool — nothing
  on this rock holds a level of its own (`tide.md`).
- **North**: the crown, highest ground, and the tower.

## The massing, bottom to top

Six ground planes, and they are not one plane. **A composition that lays these
out at a single level has not built Halgrave.**

| stage | floor | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| the flat | −1.2 | tidal sand, cairns, iron stakes, the causeway running off south |
| the ward | −1.5 floor, +0.4 causeway top | the drowned outer ward, a basin **below** the gate that admits it |
| the gate | +2.5 | the gatehouse passage, in the rock's south foot |
| the road | +4.0 | the cut ledge on the west cliff, above and outside everything |
| the cloister | +9 | the chapel ward's paving, on the upper shelf |
| the hall | +12 | the keep's floor, the one intact roof |
| the ramp and belfry | +14 / +30 | the upper ward's cobbles, and the belfry floor above them |

The cistern is under the upper half of all of this, floor at −0.15, its well's
silt bed at −2.3.

Three facts of the massing that a plan drawn from boxes loses:

1. **The ward is a hole, not a floor.** Its floor is four metres *below* the gate
   sill that opens onto it, which is the whole reason it has been sea for
   fifty-one years. Drawn level with the gate it is a courtyard and the campaign
   has no drowned ward.
2. **The cistern is beneath, not beside.** Its collapse shaft is a hole in the
   ground of the upper ward and its grille comes out in the ward's rock face 1.3
   above the ward floor, at the height the water used to stand. Drawn as a room
   next to the cloister, the shaft has nothing above it to be a hole in.
3. **The tower is a climb, not a room.** `tide.md` puts its approach at +14 and
   its belfry floor at +30: sixteen metres of tower between a player's feet at
   the door and the floor they ring from, with a broken first flight in it. A
   tower whose interior is a chain of rooms at one level is not this tower, under
   the name the campaign is called after.

## The silhouette

Judged at playable scale. A cathedral is a cathedral-sized cathedral, and the
silhouette carries the recognition that detail cannot — so this brief describes
shapes and never tracery.

Standing on the flat at the causeway head, looking north, the player sees five
bands stacked:

1. **Sand and cairns**, and the iron stakes standing in them.
2. **The gate front** — a squat, wide, pale block set into the rock's foot, one
   arched opening in it, its roof flat and walkable. To its left the cliff goes
   up dark and unbroken, with the thin horizontal scratch of the road across it.
3. **A broken toothed line** — the curtain wall of the outer ward, and over it,
   at the far side, the peaked metal roof of the water-gate tower standing out of
   water.
4. **A second broken line, higher and paler** — the cloister's roofless arcading,
   its arch-heads open to the sky, and behind it the one solid dark shape on the
   rock: the hall's roof, the only roof that is still on.
5. **The tower**, standing clear of all of it, twice the height of anything else,
   plain and square and unbuttressed, with a square hole through its top storey
   that the sky shows through — and something black inside the hole.

Everything below the tower steps; the tower does not. That contrast is the
recognition and it is the one thing the reference sheet exists to fix.

## Grandeur is what happens in it

Four volumes on this site are large. Each is large because of what is in it, and
none is large because it encloses air.

| volume | what fills it |
|---|---|
| the flat | the cairn field, one heap per body recovered, thinning inland and crowding at the tide-line; the tide-stake line as a lethal boundary; the tutorial kill; the lampman's shelf; and, at the Dead Ebb, the ground below the stake line and the several hundred standing on it |
| the drowned ward | the causeway spine and its two weed pinches; two arcades climbable end to end as an upper route; the water-gate tower's storeys; the sunk boats and the ferry-tally in one of them; and at the Dead Ebb the entire floor as new ground |
| the cistern | ranked piers that make the plan legible from any bay; the collapse-shaft's rubble cone as climbable terrain; the supply channel as a lethal line across a floor that otherwise reads uniform; the Choir's echo vault; the well head and its apron; and the silt bed the sea uncovers |
| the tower | the ramp approach between the collapsed low buildings; the tower foot; the broken first flight and the rope that bridges it; the ringing floor; the louvre stage; and a belfry that is a room walked **around** a bell, not a shelf a bell sits on |

**Mercy is built, not placed.** It is a structure of oxidised copper in a timber
frame that is part of the building, large enough that the belfry's clear internal
size is set by the bell plus a walking ring around it. The vanilla block that
shares its name would be a fitting on a fence post; placing it would label the
object instead of depicting it.

## What the belfry has to see

From the belfry floor at +30 the player looks down on the whole rock at the
lowest water in fifty-one years, and every zone they have crossed is visible and
changed. That is a constraint on the plan, not a piece of prose: **the site is a
compact stepped mass, not a chain.** Z0, Z1, Z2, Z3, Z4, Z5 and the cistern's
collapse shaft must all fall inside one downward view from the tower's top. A
layout that strings the zones end to end satisfies every route in `quests.md` and
loses this.

## The state the reference is drawn at

The **standing tide**, `0.0`. One plane, whole-world, at the same height in the
ward, around the rock and over the sand, because it is one sea and not a set of
pools. At this level the flat is under, the ward is chest-deep, the cistern is
ankle-deep and the gate passage's channel is a dry gutter. Nothing on the site is
lower than anything else in a way that would let one body of water drain into
another.

## What the produced pieces actually are

Four of the eight zones have been produced. `../GENERATION.md` and the review
sets record what came out, and it is not always what the image promised. Stated
here as fact, because a whole-map reference drawn from the images alone would be
drawn from a place that does not exist yet.

- **Z0 barrow shore** — produced by the tidal-keep generator before the prefab
  procedure existed, owner-reviewed and accepted with a shoreline-edge
  correction. Its declared region is 19 x 6 x 24.
- **Z1 cliff road** — produced. `GENERATION.md` records two things the program
  cannot express: **the ledge does not overhang** (the walking surface is flush
  with the rock below it, so the seaward elevation is a flat plane with a seam,
  where the concept's road projects on a lip over an undercut drop), and **the
  concept's iron stanchions and their chain rail have no role and no rule** —
  which is the element beat 1.2 is built on and the tell 初见杀 K2 is read from.
  The exterior review shot `review/z1/sea-front.png` is a flat slab of dark rock
  with a horizontal seam.
- **Z2 gatehouse** — produced at 20 x 10 x 84 and awaiting review. Its interior
  review set reads as a vaulted passage with a channel down it. Its exterior shot
  `review/z2/ext-se.png` is a long flat-topped rectangular box. The set's own
  README records that the passage's approach face renders as flat grey because
  the renderer has nothing beyond it to draw, and that the view out through the
  gateway can only be judged once the zones are placed together.
- **Z4 chapel ward** — produced at 16 x 9 x 26 and awaiting review.
  `GENERATION.md` states the divergence plainly: *"The concept image is an open,
  roofless cloister; the zone's design intent and topology are an enclosed hub.
  The palette and proportions converge on the concept's material and tone, and
  the composition does not — deliberately, the topology is fixed."* 47.9% of the
  piece is inert `margin`. `review/z4/top.png` is a solid roofed rectangle with
  no open garth.

Z3, Z5, Z6 and Z7 have programs exported and are unproduced. `Z3` is the
campaign's one open machine finding: `fluid-contained` reds on it with `DW0800`,
380 ways out of a 2304-cell body of water, and `GENERATION.md` records that the
answer is a design answer about where the ward's water is walled and where it is
meant to spill.

**This brief describes Halgrave, not the produced pieces.** The four notes above
are what a reader needs in order to know which of the two they are looking at.

## The reference

This brief is drawn as **four separate full-frame views**, each generated on its
own so that a view which comes back wrong is re-rolled by itself:

| view | what it fixes |
|---|---|
| `map-view1-front.jpg` | the front elevation from the causeway head — the five-band silhouette above, and the recognition |
| `map-view2-west.jpg` | the west seaward face: sheer undercut cliff to surf, and the one cut road-ledge across it |
| `map-view3-plan.jpg` | the straight-down site plan, north at the top — the view a composition program is written from |
| `map-view4-aerial.jpg` | the compact stepped mass as one object, and the ward read as a basin sunk below the gate |

`map-prompt.md` holds how they are made, the exact commands, the frame each view
is given and why, and the places where the four disagree. The prompt of record
for each view, the shared text and the provider responses are committed beside
them.

**Mercy's scale is fixed by the paragraph above and not by any picture.** The
belfry is drawn as a square opening on every face with the sky through it and the
bell hung in the middle of it, which is what *built, not hung as an ornament*
means; how much of that opening the bell fills in a given view is a drawing, and
the clear internal size set by the bell plus a walking ring around it is the
binding statement.

All four views draw the crag as natural rock with a curved shoreline, an undercut
cliff and sloping turf, and draw the ward's ring wall as a curve. The grammar
back end has no smooth curve, no diagonal and no terrain. That is named in
`map-prompt.md` as a triage item rather than quietly redrawn out of the
reference, because simplifying a reference to fit the tool is the same defect
this brief exists to prevent, arriving through the front door.
