THE VIEW — A THREE-QUARTER AERIAL OF HALGRAVE, FROM THE SOUTH-WEST.

Draw the whole rock as ONE COMPACT OBJECT, seen from high above the sea to the SOUTH-WEST, looking down and away to the north-east, in sea fog. High enough that the eye looks down INTO the flooded ward and down onto the roofs, low enough that the bell tower still stands up against the fog and the sky rather than being seen from above.

The one fact this view exists to carry: EVERYTHING FITS INSIDE ONE DOWNWARD LOOK. The sand flat and its cairns, the gatehouse, the flooded ward with its water and its arcades and its water-gate tower, the cloister, the hall, the bell tower, and the black collapse hole in the upper ward's paving are ALL VISIBLE AT ONCE, close together, packed tightly one above the next up a small rock. The site is a COMPACT STEPPED MASS, not a chain — never strung out end to end, never a row of separate buildings with ground between them. It is a small rock with a great deal on it.

From this angle:

- The wet sand flat and its cairn field and stake lines lie in the near foreground at the bottom of the frame, with the causeway running off toward the viewer's side.
- The gatehouse sits at the sand's head, low, its flat roof read from above.
- Behind and BELOW it the drowned ward is plainly a BASIN SUNK INTO THE ROCK: the eye looks down into a hole full of grey sea water, four metres below the gate that opens onto it, with its arcades and the raised causeway spine standing in the water and the boats half sunk beside them. The breach in its east wall is open to the sea and the water is continuous through it. Low in the ward's inner rock face, just above the water, one small dark iron grille.
- Above the ward, the cloister's roofless square, its open court seen down into.
- Above that the hall's steep dark roof, the one intact roof, seen from above and behind.
- The cobbled ramp climbing north-east between the low broken walls of the upper ward, and beside it in the paving the one black broken hole.
- And at the crown, clear of everything and touching nothing, the bell tower — pale, square, plain, rising far above every other thing on the rock, its square belfry opening showing sky and the enormous dark bell through it.

The rock's west side, nearest the viewer's left, falls away as sheer dark undercut cliff to surf, with the one thin cut road-ledge scratched across it.


THE SUBJECT — HALGRAVE. The same place in every image of this series.

A ruined fortified priory standing on a low oval tidal crag off a drowned northern coast, an hour's walk from the mainland across wet sand. The crag's long axis runs south-west to north-east. Compass, and it is fixed: the causeway and the mainland are SOUTH; the sheer seaward cliff is WEST; the rock's crown and the bell tower are NORTH; the outer ward's breached sea-wall is EAST.

The rock steps up from south to north in five stages, and the stages must read as clearly DIFFERENT HEIGHTS stacked one above another, never as one flat level. Heights are metres against the sea surface, which is 0 and is the same single sea everywhere in the picture.

1. THE TIDAL FLAT, ground at −1.2, just awash: bare grey wet sand, scattered small drystone burial cairns, a line of thin rusted iron stakes standing in the sand, and a causeway of wet sand running away south out of the picture toward the mainland.

2. THE GATEHOUSE, floor +2.5: a squat, wide, low block of pale ashlar built into the rock's south foot at the head of the causeway, with ONE arched gateway in it facing south down onto the sand, and a flat walkable platform for a roof. It is the only gate in the rock.

3. THE DROWNED OUTER WARD, floor −1.5: immediately behind (north of) the gatehouse and SUNK FOUR METRES BELOW the gate sill that opens onto it. It is a HOLE IN THE ROCK, a basin, never a courtyard — and it is full of standing sea water because it is lower than the sea. A broken curtain wall rings it, breached on its EAST side so that the open sea comes straight in through the gap. Two runs of ruined stone arcading stand up out of the water; a raised stone causeway spine crosses the water with its top at +0.4, barely proud of the surface; half-sunk open boats lie in it; and at its far north-east corner a small square water-gate tower with a peaked metal roof and shuttered windows stands on its own base in the water.

4. THE CLOISTER, paving +9: on the rock shelf above the ward and north-west of it, a roofless cloister — four ranges of broken arcading round an open paved court with grass in the joints, every arch-head open to the sky.

5. THE HALL AND KEEP, floor +12: north of the cloister and higher again, the ONE intact roofed building on the rock — a long stone hall, its long axis running east–west, with a steep dark roof and a squat keep at its end. It is the only solid dark shape on the rock below the tower.

6. THE UPPER WARD AND THE RAMP, +14: a cobbled ramp climbing north-east between the low broken walls of a collapsed upper ward, to the tower's foot. In the paving of that upper ward there is one dark broken HOLE where a brick vault beneath the rock has collapsed. The vault itself is underground and is never drawn; that hole is its only evidence.

AND AT THE CROWN, set back north of the hall on the highest ground, standing clear of everything and abutting nothing: A TALL SQUARE BELL TOWER of pale weathered stone, plain and unbuttressed, with a flat top and no spire. Its foot stands at +14 and its belfry floor at +30 — sixteen metres of blank wall between them. Its top storey is an OPEN BELFRY: one large SQUARE opening on every face with the sky showing straight through, and inside that opening a single ENORMOUS dark bronze bell hung in a heavy timber frame, so large it very nearly fills the opening. The tower rises far higher than anything else on the rock. Everything below the tower steps upward; the tower does not step, it rises.

THE WEST FACE of the rock is sheer dark undercut cliff falling to surf and rock teeth, unclimbable, with ONE thin cut road-ledge scratched horizontally across it about four metres above the sea. That single scratch is the only break in the cliff.

THE SILHOUETTE, which must survive at small size: one pale square tower with a hole of sky at its top, standing on a stepped grey rock, with a dark band of drowned ruin around its foot and flat wet sand around that.

THE WATER is ONE SINGLE SEA at ONE SINGLE LEVEL everywhere in the picture — the same height inside the flooded ward, around the rock, and over the sand flat. It is never a pool and never a separate basin. At this level the sand flat is just awash, the ward is chest deep, and the water standing in the ward is at exactly the same height as the sea outside it.

DRAWING: painted digital concept art, loose confident brushwork, flat cold overcast daylight, sea fog thinning the distance, no sun and no cast shadows. Massing and silhouette only. No carved tracery, no mouldings, no window detail, no ornament of any kind. No people or figures, no birds, no banners, no boats afloat. No colour outside grey, grey-green, cold blue-grey and rust brown.

THIS IS ONE SINGLE FULL-FRAME IMAGE OF ONE VIEW. It is not a sheet, not a grid, not a set of panels, and it carries no inset, no border, no gutter, no label and no text. Draw the one view named at the top of this prompt, filling the frame edge to edge, and draw no other view of the place anywhere in it.
