SUBJECT — Halgrave: a fortified priory on a tidal rock off a drowned northern coast. One place, drawn as one object.

A low oval crag of dark grey rock standing out of a wide flat of wet grey tidal sand. Its long axis runs south-west to north-east. The rock STEPS UP from south to north, and each step carries one part of the citadel, stacked ABOVE the step before it. This is a stepped mass, not a row of buildings on one level. From the bottom up, with heights in metres against the sea:

- THE FLAT, at -1.2: wide open wet grey tidal sand, JUST AWASH AT THE STANDING TIDE — a sheet of shallow standing water lying over grey sand, wet and mirror-flat, reflecting the overcast, never a dry beach and never an exposed foreshore above a waterline — with drystone burial cairns heaped on it and a line of rusted iron tide-stakes standing UP OUT OF the shallow water, sea fog closing it in the distance.
- THE CLIFF ROAD, at +4: one narrow cut ledge climbing north across the sheer west sea-cliff, a single thin line of worked rock on an otherwise unbroken dark face, with two dark cave mouths on it.
- THE GATEHOUSE, at +2.5: a squat, wide, pale stone block built into the rock's south foot at the head of the causeway, one arched opening in its south front, flat walkable roof, and a broken sea-torn hole in its outer wall where the cliff road ends.
- THE DROWNED WARD, floor at -1.5: a wide roofless outer ward behind and BELOW the gate, FULL OF STANDING SEA WATER, ringed by a broken toothed curtain wall, its east sea-wall breached open to the sea, two ruined stone arcades standing in the water, a raised stone causeway crossing it just above the water at +0.4, sunk boats off the causeway, and, WHERE THAT RAISED CAUSEWAY ENDS at the ward's far side away from the gatehouse, a small square water-gate tower of three low storeys with a peaked dark metal PYRAMID roof and shuttered windows, standing on its own base in the water and rising well clear above both the water and the broken curtain wall. It is never at the near end beside the gatehouse.
- THE CLOISTER, at +9: a roofless cloister on the shelf above the ward, arcade ranges on four sides, their arch-heads open to the sky, grass in the paving joints.
- THE HALL AND KEEP, at +12: the one intact roofed building on the rock, long axis east-west, steep dark pitched roof, slit windows. The only solid dark shape below the tower.
- THE UPPER WARD AND RAMP, at +14: cobbles between collapsed low buildings on the crown, with a black collapse hole in the paving where a cistern vault below has fallen in.
- THE BELL TOWER: plain, square, pale, unbuttressed, standing clear of everything and set back on the crown, its foot at +14 and its belfry floor at +30 — twice the height of anything else on the rock. Its top storey is pierced by a large square opening on every face that the sky shows through, and an enormous dark bell of oxidised copper in a heavy timber frame is BUILT into that opening and nearly fills it. The belfry is a room walked around the bell, not a shelf the bell sits on.

The rock's whole west and south-west face is sheer dark unclimbable cliff falling to surf and rock teeth. The sea is ONE flat plane at height 0.0 everywhere — around the rock, over the sand, and inside the drowned ward. Nothing holds a level of its own.

LIGHT AND HAND: flat cold overcast North Sea daylight, sea fog, no sun, no cast shadows, no warm colour anywhere. Muted grey and grey-green, desaturated painted concept art. Massing and silhouette carry the recognition; no tracery, no fine ornament, no figures, no birds.

THIS VIEW SPECIFICALLY — and the cut road is the subject of this drawing, not a detail on it:

The whole west face is one sheer dark cliff of unclimbable rock falling into surf and standing rock teeth. Across that face, and this is the most important thing in the picture, runs THE CLIFF ROAD: a single narrow ledge cut into the rock, one person wide, unmistakable, running as a continuous line from the LOWER RIGHT of the cliff to the UPPER LEFT. It begins at the tidal sand at the cliff's south foot, which is on the RIGHT, climbs steadily north across the whole width of the face, and turns into the rock at the top LEFT. It is a ROUTE that crosses the entire elevation, from one edge of the rock to the other — not a short scratch, not a broken mark, not a texture in the stone.

On the road, in order from its low south end to its high north end:
- a stretch of ledge so narrow that one person fills it;
- a row of rusted iron brackets driven into the rock wall at hand height above the ledge, the anchors of a rope handrail that is long gone, some of them bent down or missing;
- a gap where the shelf under the missing brackets has fallen away into the sea, leaving a straight drop, at a blind bend;
- two dark cave mouths one after the other, where the road passes through the rock: the rope store;
- at the top LEFT, the road ends at a broken sea-torn hole in the pale masonry of the gatehouse's outer wall, high above the sand.

Above the road, higher up the rock and set back: the roofless cloister's arcading, the hall's dark pitched roof, and clear above everything the plain square bell tower with its square belfry opening and the great dark bell built into it.

Below the road: nothing but sheer rock, surf, and the sea at one flat level. On the RIGHT of the frame, the wet grey tidal sand of the flat runs south with cairns on it and is closed by sea fog. The left of the frame is closed by sea fog too.
