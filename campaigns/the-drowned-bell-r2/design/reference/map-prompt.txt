A single reference sheet: ONE image divided into FOUR clearly separated panels by clean pale gutters, each panel a different view of the SAME place. Do not blend the panels together and do not draw four different buildings. It is one island, drawn four ways.

PANEL LAYOUT, exactly this:
- TOP LEFT: front elevation, orthographic, the island seen straight on from the south at sea level.
- TOP RIGHT: side elevation, orthographic, the island seen straight on from the west at sea level.
- BOTTOM LEFT: a FLAT MAP of the island. This panel is a map, NOT a view: the camera points straight down, vertically, from directly overhead. No horizon, no sky, no fog and no perspective in this panel at all. Roofs, paving, water and sand are seen from above as flat shapes; the island's outline reads as a silhouette on the water; the tower appears as a small square roof casting no shadow. North is at the top. Draw it like an architect's site plan painted in the same palette.
- BOTTOM RIGHT: three-quarter aerial view, the whole island from the south-west, high up, in sea fog.

THE SUBJECT, identical in all four panels: HALGRAVE, a ruined fortified priory standing on a small tidal rock off a drowned northern coast, in flat grey overcast daylight, painted in a muted grey, grey-green and rust-brown palette.

The rock is a low oval crag rising out of a wide flat of grey tidal sand, its long axis running south-west to north-east. Its west face is sheer dark cliff falling to surf and rock teeth. It steps up from south to north in five stages, and the stages must read as clearly DIFFERENT HEIGHTS stacked above one another, never as one flat level:

1. At the south foot, at sand level: a bare tidal flat scattered with small drystone burial cairns and a line of thin rusted iron stakes, with a causeway of wet sand running south out of the frame toward the mainland.

2. At the north edge of the sand: a squat, wide gatehouse block of pale ashlar built into the rock's foot, one arched gateway in it facing south down onto the sand, its roof a flat walkable platform. On the rock's west cliff above the surf, a thin cut road-ledge scratched horizontally across the rock face.

3. Behind the gatehouse and LOWER than it, sunk into the rock: a wide roofless outer ward FLOODED with standing sea water, ringed by a broken curtain wall that the sea has breached on the east side. Two runs of ruined stone arcading stand in the water; a raised stone causeway crosses the water; half-sunk open boats lie in it; and at the far side a small square water-gate tower with a peaked metal roof and shuttered windows stands on its own base in the water.

4. On the rock shelf above the ward: a roofless cloister, four ranges of broken pointed arcading around an open paved court with grass in its joints.

5. Above the cloister: the one intact roofed building on the island, a long stone hall with a steep dark roof and a squat keep at its end.

AND AT THE CROWN, standing above and behind everything else, the thing the whole silhouette is for: a tall square bell tower of pale weathered stone, plain and unbuttressed, rising far higher than the hall's roof. Its top storey is an OPEN BELFRY: a large square opening on every face with sky visible straight through it, and hanging inside that opening a single ENORMOUS dark bronze bell in a heavy timber frame, a bell so large it nearly fills the belfry. A cobbled ramp climbs to the tower's foot between the low ruined walls of a collapsed upper ward, and in the paving of that upper ward there is a dark broken hole where a vault beneath has collapsed.

THE SILHOUETTE, which must survive at small size: one pale square tower with a hole of sky at its top, standing on a stepped grey rock, with a dark band of drowned ruin around its foot and flat wet sand around that. Everything below the tower steps upward; the tower stands clear and does not.

THE WATER is one single sea at one single level in every panel: the same height in the flooded ward, around the rock, and over the sand. It is not a pool and never a separate basin.

DRAWING: painted digital concept art, loose confident brushwork, flat cold overcast light, sea fog thinning the distance, no sun and no cast shadows. Massing and silhouette only. No carved tracery, no mouldings, no window detail, no ornament of any kind. No people or figures, no boats afloat, no birds, no banners. No colour outside grey, grey-green, cold blue-grey and rust brown. Keep the four panels clearly separated by gutters.
