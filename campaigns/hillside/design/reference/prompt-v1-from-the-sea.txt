View 1, Saltstair seen from the sea, from a boat some sixty blocks south of the quay, at eye height just above the water, in late afternoon. The whole town fills the frame. In front, the quay: a straight stone wall rising about two blocks out of the water, with square timber bollards, a long low stone warehouse with a slate roof at its right-hand end, and a small sand beach at its left-hand end. Behind the quay the town climbs the hill in five terraces, each about four blocks higher than the last, joined by one main street that runs straight up the slope from the middle of the quay to a small square at the very top: at each terrace the street is a flat stretch of cobbles, and between terraces it is a broad flight of stone steps. Eight buildings stand along the street, four on each side, each on its own terrace so their roofs step up the hill like stairs: near the quay the harbourmaster's narrow house on the left and the warehouse on the right; above them a long rope-walk shed on the right; a merchant's tall house with a steep roof on the left; a smithy with a chimney on the left and a weaver's house on the right; and at the top square a small stone chapel with a short tower on the left and a wide inn with lit windows on the right. Low stone retaining walls hold each terrace above the one below, and stepped turf hillside shows between and beside the houses. Behind and above the town, the green hill of the peninsula rises and runs away to the north under a pale sky; open sea on both sides of the town.
