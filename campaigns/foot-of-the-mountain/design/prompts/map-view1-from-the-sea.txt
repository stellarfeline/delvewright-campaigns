View 1, Kelderby seen from the sea, from a boat some eighty blocks south of the quay, at eye height just above the water, in late afternoon light. The whole town fills the frame. In front, the harbour basin and the quay: a long straight stone wharf about two blocks out of the water with square bollards, stacked crates and nets, small fishing boats moored along it, and the harbourmaster's stone house at its right-hand (east) end. From the middle of the quay one broad main street climbs straight up the slope away from the viewer, northward, in ten short flights of shallow steps between level cobbled stretches, the houses along both sides stepping up the hill so their roofs rise like stairs; a few narrow side lanes leave it to left and right, and festival bunting crosses the street. At the top of the street is a cobbled square: on its right (east) side the tall west front of a large Gothic cathedral with flying buttresses and a tall bell tower at its north-west corner, its long nave running away to the east; on its left (west) side a large timber-framed coaching inn. Behind and above the square rises a steep green mountain, and on its summit, about eighty blocks higher than the square, stands a square grey Scottish tower-house castle with a tall gatehouse tower, a long hall range and a curtain wall, reached by a long cobbled road that zig-zags up the mountain's flank from the square. On the right of the frame a rocky green headland runs out into the sea on the east side of the harbour, a narrow cliff path along its spine, and at its far seaward tip a tall white lighthouse with a small keeper's cottage in a walled yard. Open blue sea on the left.
