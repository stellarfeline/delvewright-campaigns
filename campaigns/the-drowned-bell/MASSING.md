# Massing intent — what the owner is choosing, per zone

Companion to `REMAKE.md`. `REMAKE.md` §3 says what each zone *is*; this says what
is still **open** about its shape, so a contact sheet shows a real choice and the
owner's hour buys a decision instead of a formality.

The sweep manifests that produce those sheets live in `massing/`, one per zone,
generated by `massing/make-manifests.py`. Each candidate's comment there names
the guard clause it was solved against.

## Why this file exists

Two measurements forced it, both from the round that built the sheets.

**The seed is not an axis.** A box-split grammar picks alternatives by guards on
the box's dimensions and touches the RNG only where two alternatives apply at
once. Measured over 32 seeds per zone: seven of eight zones produce one building,
five of them pixel-identical. Five zones' own fixture notes already said so —
*"nothing in the gatehouse draws from the seed; it is stated, not chosen"* — and
"seed-varied candidates" survived in the prose because nobody re-read it against
the programs. A sheet built on seeds would have shown six copies of one building
and asked which was preferred.

**Parameters alone are usually refused.** The zones guard their own shapes. A
single-parameter sweep mostly hits a refusal, which is the guard working. **So
each row below names the region change that must move with the parameter** — that
is what makes a candidate legal rather than refused.

A candidate is therefore a **variation**: region, parameters, or seed. Never seed
alone.

## How a candidate is chosen now

A refusal states which clause was false, what each side evaluated to, what fed
each operand, and how far short it fell. Every guard in these zones is a linear
inequality over the box's own dimensions and the program's parameters, so **the
next candidate is a deduction and not a guess**: read the inequality, solve it,
carry the region change it demands. The four thin zones went 2/2/3/3 → 6/6/6/6
in one pass this way, with one arithmetic slip caught by the reading that named
it ("20 short: the left must rise to 20").

## Three facts about the boxes that every zone inherits

Learned by measurement in the round that raised the thin zones, and each of them
refused a candidate before it was understood.

1. **The box is normalised before it is expanded.** Every zone's frame is
   `Reorient::KEEP.y(WorldY).z(Largest)`, so the local `Z` is whichever
   *horizontal* axis is longer. A region and its transpose are the **same
   building at ninety degrees**: `[19,6,24]` and `[24,6,19]` both expand as
   19 x 6 x 24, with identical `filled_cells`. To widen a zone, raise its
   *shorter* horizontal dimension; to lengthen it, raise the longer.
2. **`distinct_massings` does not see that rotation.** It is computed from the
   world-space solid/air bitmap, so a transposed candidate scores as distinct
   while showing the owner one building twice. Z0's sheet read 4 when it held 3
   buildings. Until the metric normalises orientation, check any two candidates
   whose regions are transposes of each other, and treat equal `filled_cells`
   across two candidates as a duplicate until proven otherwise.
3. **`elite_ground` floors the width of every zone it appears in.** Its own
   guard is `X >= radius*2 + 1 + (flank_margin*2 + 2)` = **19** at the design's
   radius 4 and flank margin 4. Z0's box is 19 wide, and Z3's and Z6's mainlines
   are 19 wide, because that is the floor and not a preference. Combined with
   the frame rule that every run must *exceed* the mainline, it produces a hard
   floor of **20 cells on every named run** in those zones — see Z3 and Z6.

## What is *not* on the table

Not offered as choices, and a sheet that appears to offer them is a defect:

- Anything `REMAKE.md` marks owner-verbatim. Z1's knockback-niche cliff path is
  mandatory vocabulary; its niches teach → test → twist across three, and a later
  leg's niche must be readable from an earlier one. That is the fairness of the
  set piece, not a preference.
- Which pieces compose a zone. That is §4's catalogue and it is settled.
- Anything that moves a bonfire's reign relative to a hostile's onset. `DW0478`
  governs it and content does not get to trade it away for a silhouette.
- **`elite_ground`'s `flank_margin` and the arena's `seal_flank`.** Narrowing the
  flank is the only lever that would buy a shorter run in Z0, Z3 and Z6, and it
  is the arena's own "the fight is optional" guarantee — the thing `ring/
  seal_flank` and `arena/seal_flank` are the teeth for. It is a gate, not a knob.
  `radius` *is* offered, because a bigger circle is a bigger fight and the
  guarantee scales with it.

## The eight zones

Each row: **the decision in play terms** — what a player would feel differently —
then the knobs, then the region change that has to accompany them.

### Z0 Barrow Shore — landing, class pick, BF1, dormant Barrow Warden

**Decision: how exposed the landing feels before the first fire.** Whether the
graves read as a field the party crosses or a cluster they walk past.

`barrow_shore` declares **no parameters at all** — it is wholly determined by its
box. That is why it measured 0 pixels of variation across 32 seeds: not a defect,
the correct behaviour of a zone with nothing to vary. **Its only axis is the
region.**

Region: the design box's 19 **is** `elite_ground`'s width floor (fact 3), so the
grave field can be widened and **cannot be narrowed** — the two 15-wide
candidates of the first pass were refused there, not at the zone. Length floors
at 17 (`Z >= radius*2 + 1 + approach*2`). Candidates span 19x24 → 26x28 with one
taller box, and remember fact 1: `[26,6,28]` is a *wider* field, `[19,6,34]` a
longer one.

The Warden is dormant and optional; a wider box is what lets a first-time player
walk past it, which is the choice.

### Z1 Cliff Road — the owner's set piece

**Decision: how far the drop reads, and how much margin the ledge leaves.**

Knobs: `fall` (≥ 8, guarded), `sea` (≥ 3, guarded), `ledge_shelf`, `turn_run`.

Region: raising `fall` needs Y; widening `sea` needs X. Both are refused inside
the current box, so **every candidate here is a region change carrying a
parameter change**.

**The switchback has landed**, and this zone is now in curation. The hold that
stood here — "as programmed it is a single run, and a single run has no earlier
leg to read the next niche from" — is discharged: `cliff_road` splits at
`turn_run` into the hairpin's head and the legs below it, so the observability
the set piece rests on exists to be judged. `turn_run` (≥ `MIN_TURN`) joins the
knob list as the length of the head the road turns on.

### Z2 Gatehouse + Outer Ward — timed portcullis, watch bay, BF2, Gate Reeve

**Decision: how long the party is under the portcullis, and how far the bypass
loop runs before it comes back.**

The two halves are governed by two different guard families, and the first pass
conflated them — it moved the six named runs and left the portcullis itself at
its default in all six candidates.

**Under the portcullis** is the gated passage, and the gated passage is the
**remainder**: `gate_run = Z - (shaft_run + motif_run + tee_run + stair_run +
stand_run + door_run)`. Its own guard is `Z >= gate/approach + gate/span + 4`. So
the felt quantities are `gate/span` (the cells crossed under it) and
`gate/approach` (how far off it is read from, floored at `MIN_STANDOFF` = 6), and
**both are bought with the zone's `Z`, one cell for one**, through the remainder.
Candidates span span 3 → 10 and approach 8 → 16.

**The bypass loop** is `tee_run` against `strip_depth`, locked from both ends:

    strip_depth > tee_run                    (else the bar's wall turns along the ward)
    tee_run > Dimension.X - strip_depth      (the mainline frame guard)

Substituting gives `2 * strip_depth > X`, so the tightest legal loop at a given
width is `strip_depth = (X + 2) / 2` — which at X = 20 is exactly the design's
11/10. **The design box is already at its own minimum loop**: a shorter loop is a
narrower zone and nothing else, and below X = 14 the mainline falls under
`disarm_stand::MIN_WIDTH` (6) and the stand refuses. `shaft/drop` sets the plinth
and `Y >= shaft/drop + MIN_UPPER` guards it, so a deeper drop must come with
more Y.

The watch bay's line of sight to the full portcullis cycle is an obligation
(§4 entry O), not a variable — a candidate that shortens the bay until the cycle
is not observable is wrong however it looks.

### Z3 Drowned Lower Ward — the toll road, flooded ward

**Decision: how long the wade is before the far-side bar.**

**The wade is the causeway, and the causeway is the remainder piece** —
`crossing_run = Z - ward_run - junction_run`. This file previously named
`ward_run` as the wade; that is wrong. `ward_run` is the lower ward's **arena**
(`elite_ground`), the fight *after* the bar. Travel runs local Z-max → Z-min, so
the party wades the remainder first, meets the junction with the bar in its
flank, and comes out onto the arena floor.

Knobs: `Z` (the wade, through the remainder), `ward_run`, `junction_run`,
`strip_depth`, `ring/radius`.

Region: the four guards are `strip_depth > junction_run` and
`ward_run`, `junction_run`, `crossing_run` each `> X - strip_depth`. By fact 3
the mainline is pinned at 19, so **the wade cannot be shorter than 20 cells at
the design box's ward width.** The reading is two-sided and both doors are shut:

    crossing_run > mainline      16 > 19    4 short          (the zone's frame)
    then narrow the mainline to 15 to allow it, and:
    ring: X >= radius*2+1+(flank_margin*2+2)   15 >= 19   4 short   (the arena)

Widening is free: candidates span the wade 20 → 56 at the design width, plus a
deeper shortcut strip (`strip_depth` 27, which leaves `junction_run` exactly 22)
and one `ring/radius` 6 ward whose mainline floor rises to 23.

### Z4 Chapel Ward (hub) — the hearth

**Decision: whether the hearth reads as a room the party rests in or an alcove
they pass.**

Knobs: `hearth_run`, `junction_run`, `strip_depth`.

Region: the mainline had to widen to 7 for the nook to exist at all, so the
hearth's size trades directly against the hub's width. This is the zone where
the decision is most nearly a pure parameter — `hearth_run` against a fixed box
is legal across a real range. A smaller hearth is bought with a **narrower
mainline**, not a smaller number, and the mainline floors at 6.

As the hub, its junction count is fixed by the topology in `REMAKE.md` §3. Do not
offer candidates that change how many ways lead out of it.

### Z5 Great Hall + Keep — rafters, stores, bait gallery, Hall Marshal

**Decision: the hall's proportion — a long nave or a broad room — and how much
gallery the bait sits in.**

Knobs: `gallery_run`, `door_run`, `motif_run`, `store_run`, `duct_run`,
`duct/drop`, and `X` itself.

Z5 has **no branch strip**, so its frame guard is measured against the zone's own
width and not a mainline's: all five named runs *and* the remainder `hall_run`
must be `> Dimension.X`. That one fact refused three of the first pass's six, and
it has a consequence worth stating plainly: **a broader keep lengthens every room
in it.** Widening X by 4 raises the floor under six things at once, so
`broad-room` is not X + 4 — it is X + 4 and Z + 20.

The second binding guard is `rafter_hall`'s density cap over the hall's own
interior, `(X-2) * Z * beam_period >= 24*Z + 24*beam_period`, which at
`beam_period` 4 reduces to `Z >= 96 / (4*(X-2) - 24)`. So **a narrow hall must be
long to earn its rafters**: at X = 9 the hall needs 24 cells or the truss
alternative stops applying and the plan refuses outright (the plain-hall
alternative only applies under `Y < 6`, which is not the case above the plinth).

Candidates span hall proportion 1.07 (nearly square, X 15) → 3.64 (long nave,
X 11), with a 2.67 narrow keep, a 32-cell gallery, and the deepened plinth
(`duct/drop` 8, which needs `Y` 15: `bait_stand` still wants `head + 2` = 7
above the plinth).

Entry B's rule holds: the bait and its watcher must both be legible from the same
places. A candidate that lengthens the gallery until the watcher leaves the
frame has broken the pattern, not varied it.

### Z6 Cistern Deep — dart gallery, grate elite

**Decision: how long the dart gallery runs before the arena opens.**

Knobs: `gallery_run`, `arena_run`, `vent_run`, `sally_run`, `strip_depth`,
`arena/radius`.

Same shape as Z3, same cause. Every mainline frame guard measures against
`X - strip_depth`, and fact 3 pins that at 19, so all four named runs and the
shaft's remainder must be at least 20 and **the dart gallery cannot be shortened
below 20 at the design box's arena width.** Both doors shut, as in Z3:

    gallery_run > mainline    16 > 19   4 short         (the zone's frame)
    narrow the mainline to 15, and the arena refuses:  15 >= 19   4 short

**`arena_run` is not the size of the arena.** `elite_ground` spends any `Z` over
its minimum on the *exit run*, never on the circle — so a genuinely bigger fight
is `arena/radius`, and that raises the mainline's own floor with it (radius 6
wants 23, hence 24-cell runs and a 25-deep strip). Candidates span the gallery
20 → 56, plus a radius-6 arena, a 26-cell sally branch, and a doubled fall.

This is the zone the cutaway work was measured on — its top two courses are
solid, so judge it on `plan-mid` and `sec-z`, never on `top`, which showed 0.00%
of frame for a five-course headroom change.

### Z7 Bell Tower — ascent, loft, Bellkeeper

**Decision: how long the climb is, and how much room the last fight has.**

Knobs: `climb`, `ring_run`, `loft_run`, `flight/tread`, `flight/landing_run`,
`flight/head`, `shaft/storey`, `shaft/sill`, `hearth_run`, `tee_run`,
`door_run`, `strip_depth`.

Region: `climb` is bound by identity to the flight's tread count and to the
shaft's sill —

    climb == (Z - (ring+door+tee+loft+hearth) - landing_run*2) / tread
    shaft/sill == climb,  and  Y >= climb + flight/head + 1

— so `Z = 2*climb + sum(runs) + 6` at the default tread 2 and landing_run 3, and
**raising the climb is always a Y change**.

**There is no tight-ring candidate, and that is a finding rather than an
omission.** `ring_run` is squeezed from both sides — `> X - strip_depth` from the
frame, and `>= radius*2 + 1 + approach*2` from the belfry — while the belfry also
floors the mainline itself at `radius*2 + 1 + flank_margin*2 + 2`. Widening the
strip to narrow the mainline therefore starves the ring before it shortens it,
and shrinking the radius raises the mainline's floor faster than it lowers the
run's. **At this design box the shortest legal `ring_run` is the default: the
last fight's room can be enlarged and cannot be reduced.** Measured over five
deduced candidates. This zone ships five candidates, not six.

The most interesting candidate here is the trade between a tall climb and a
generous ring: both want the same budget, and it is a real choice about whether
the tower's memory is the ascent or the fight at the top.

## Rendering these

Judge massing on cutaways, not silhouettes. A `top` shot of a zone carved from
solid mass strips one Y layer and shows more rock — measured at 0.00% of frame
for a change that moves interior walls. `plan-mid` and `sec-z` are the shots that
carry the decision, and the sheets are built once on each:

    tools/zone-sheets.py --out <dir> --manifest-dir <campaign>/massing \
        --shot plan-mid --require-choice

A section plane is a lottery: `sec-x`/`sec-z` sit at 50%, and a change that
misses them scores zero. If a candidate looks identical, check it against
`plan-mid` before concluding the knob is inert.

**The sheet normalises each candidate to its own thumbnail**, so absolute scale
does not read across cells — a 60-long and a 96-long zone fill the same square.
Proportion reads; length does not. Where the decision is a length (Z3's wade,
Z6's gallery), read it off the *interior* — the flooded band, the gallery's lane
— against the cell's own frame, not against the neighbouring cell.

## The acceptance test for a sheet

Before any sheet reaches the owner, the sweep must report **at least four
distinct massings** for that zone (owner's standard), and every candidate on the
final run must have built — a refusal that survives to the sheet is either
deliberate and named in the manifest, or a defect.

`distinct_massings == 1` is the degenerate failure: it means the knobs chosen for
that zone are inert, and showing it spends her attention to receive no
information. But the number is only as good as its binding — see fact 2 above: a
transposed region inflates it without adding a building. Check the count and the
duplicates.

Where a zone cannot reach four in some direction, that direction is stated as a
finding with its refusal readings — Z0's width, Z3's wade, Z6's gallery and Z7's
ring are all recorded above — rather than forced with a candidate that breaks a
gate. A closed direction is information the owner needs before she chooses.
