# The Lane of Holdings

> **Requires delve engine 0.69.0 or newer** — last verified with delvec 1.15.0 on Minecraft Java 1.21.11.

Holding Lane runs out of the village to the fields. A family's house stands on
its own ground, the smith keeps his forge, a chapel stands alone on its plot
beside the same chapel built another way, and the byre waits at the lane's end
where the pasture begins.

A test level of five minutes or so, for one to four players. No fight. The byre
is the only place you must reach; look in at any holding on the way.

## Classes

- **Traveller** — a walking staff and bread.

## Playing it

Start the server, then in the Minecraft Java client at the version the marker
above names: Multiplayer → Direct Connect → the address the host gives you.
Accept the resource pack when the server offers it.

## For the owner: what to look for

This test level shows one thing: a plot is planned with what it holds. Each
holding on the lane was planned as its parts — its structures, its open ground,
which are walked — before its ground was cut, and its size follows from them.
Inside its ground each part is built in its own outline.

1. **From the lane**, do the four read as four holdings — a house with its
   yards, a forge with its work yard, a chapel, a byre and its pen?
2. **The house**: is it an L — the long house and the cart shed against its east
   wall — and does the shed read as a wing with a job (a door through from the
   house, its own door onto the back yard)?
3. **The yards**: does each answer what you would do there — the front strip
   to the door with the flower bed behind its low hedge, the back yard with its
   washing posts and vegetable rows, the forge's work yard with the grindstone
   and the wood, the pen?
4. **The forge**: the shop and workroom stand right at the lane's edge, the yard
   beside it, the iron store shut in the yard's corner. Does it read as one
   holding?
5. **The two chapels**: the left one is cut as a plot holding one structure, the
   right one as a lidded box. Do they read the same from the lane?
6. **The density**: is the lane too sparse or too crowded?
