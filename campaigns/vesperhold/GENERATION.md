# Vesperhold — generation record

## Toolchain

- Built with the engine at revision `503f7925` (`delvec 1.6.0, dsl 0.33.0, mc 1.21.11`), `dsl_version` 0.33.0 on every stage document and the `zh-cn` sidecar.
- Placement: `areas[]` with one area bound to one campaign-built piece, `prefab/vesperhold` — the whole site as a grammar program generated from `design/programs/`, ringed by a `valley` surround. A site plan was drafted first and dropped: its derived walls and roofs are the blockout's fixed palette and stay whole-owned after detail, so it cannot give the castle a designed exterior, which is the brief's one standing requirement (the Doune Castle site in this repository is the bar). The same single-area shape rules out a second space, so the second timeline is told as echoes in the present rooms.
- No shipped library piece is bound; the campaign's only piece is its own.
- The engine constitution's operating half (`CLAUDE.local.md`) was not available to this run; nothing here decides dispatch, review, merge or staging.

## What the brief pinned, and what was invented

Pinned: souls-like combat and exploration; a cryptic main plot; one huge Gothic castle with regions and a spectacular exterior; twenty or more designed places; about half of Stormveil Castle; up to four co-op players; an hour or more of play; ruined or whole at the author's choice; a second timeline ten years earlier allowed; research before authoring.

Invented: everything named in `DESIGN.md` — the bell and its price, the Undertide, the cast, the route, the echoes, the two endings.

Research consulted before authoring (ideas only): the anatomy of Stormveil Castle and the lessons of other souls castles for structure, rest spacing, shortcuts, gatekeepers and traps; FromSoftware's fragmented storytelling, recurring allies, tricksters and past-layer precedents for the story and the echoes.

Research consulted for the off-road content (ideas only; counts are cited, the density drawn from them is this record's own arithmetic):

- Optional bosses and mini-bosses per legacy dungeon, counted: Elden Ring's Stormveil Castle holds two clearly off the critical path (the Ulcerated Tree Spirit under the Liftside Chamber, the Crucible Knight in the Rampart Tower) plus two avoidable ones on it (the Grafted Scion, the Lion Guardian) — [Fextralife, Stormveil Castle](https://eldenring.wiki.fextralife.com/Stormveil+Castle) (not freely licensed: ideas only); Dark Souls' Undead Burg and Undead Parish together hold about six off-path elites (Black Knights, Havel, the Channeler, the Berenike Knight, the Fang Boar, the Hellkite's tail) — [Fandom, Dark Souls wiki](https://darksouls.fandom.com/wiki/Taurus_Demon) (CC BY-SA) and Fextralife (ideas only); Dark Souls III's High Wall of Lothric holds one (the Lothric Wyvern) — [Fextralife, High Wall of Lothric](https://darksouls3.wiki.fextralife.com/High+Wall+of+Lothric) (ideas only).
- Every off-path elite in those counts is a one-time kill whose reward is a named piece of gear or a material, never currency alone; each sits in its own pocket with ordinary enemies and at most one treasure.
- The optional-elite pattern, its bypass and its dormancy tell, and the teach / test / twist pacing of ambushes: the engine's souls dossier (`docs/notes/souls-design-language.md` §3–4, and the sources it cites), which also records that bait set pieces in the series cover an ambush with something conspicuous in frame.
- What a legacy dungeon keeps off its road, counted where a source lists it (all ideas only):
  - **Undead Burg**: the Level Design Book's study reads most of Upper Undead Burg as optional — only one route through its map is the critical path, and it estimates about half the level as voluntary exploration; every detour pays (the first merchant, a key that unlocks a shortcut to the boss, pickups lit as "glowing breadcrumbs"), and one-way drops loop the side routes back onto the critical path ([leveldesignbook.com, *Undead Burg*](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/studies/sp/undead-burg), all rights reserved).
  - **Castle Morne**: of the fifteen item sites Fextralife's walkthrough gives between the rampart grace and the Leonine Misbegotten, thirteen are off the direct path — two smithing stones, a talisman in a tower chest, a whip and a pickled neck in cells, a claymore in the armory chest, a key, darts, a torch, greases, flowers, a remedy — and two lie on it ([Fextralife, *Castle Morne*](https://eldenring.wiki.fextralife.com/Castle+Morne)). The count is the wiki's listing as fetched and summarised, not a measurement in the game.
  - **Stormveil Castle**: Fextralife lists some 85 pickups — about 24 upgrade materials, 26 pieces of gear (15 weapons and shields, 4 talismans, 4 ashes and spells, 3 armour pieces), 20 or more consumables, and keys and lore — with two optional bosses off the path, an avoidable mini-boss, and NPCs (Nepheli, Rogier) in side rooms ([Fextralife, *Stormveil Castle*](https://eldenring.wiki.fextralife.com/Stormveil+Castle)). Critics quoted by [Wikipedia, *Stormveil Castle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormveil_Castle) (CC BY-SA) say it "hides secrets in 'seemingly innocuous places'", where "players could either go straight through, or explore and be rewarded".
  - **High Wall of Lothric**: its branches hold the wyvern (a Large Titanite Shard), a Claymore on a landing, a mimic with the Deep Battle Axe, the Pus of Man (an Ember and a shard), Greirat's cell (an NPC and a ring), an optional Winged Knight, and a lift that loops back to the first bonfire ([Fextralife, *High Wall of Lothric*](https://darksouls3.wiki.fextralife.com/High+Wall+of+Lothric)).
- No source found gives a count of ordinary enemies per legacy dungeon; nothing here claims one. Nor was a named instance found of a bait *enemy* held up by terrain; the Hedge Garden's set piece is authored on the dossier's bait-item grammar (the conspicuous thing in frame, the ambushers behind it), not transcribed. The Sally Tower's is that grammar's first variant (the ambushers co-located with the bait and visible to a body that looks up — the Cathedral-of-the-Deep rooftop the dossier cites, §4.2); the Hall of Arms' is its second (the bait is the ambusher — the mimic), made fair by the dossier's rule that the ambush be discoverable from where the player decides: the one stand with a face and a sword.

The target, and which parts of it are cited:

- **Share off the road.** Cited: the Burg is about half optional, and Morne keeps thirteen of fifteen pickups off its path. Authored: Vesperhold keeps its road whole (its eighty-minute aim is the ceiling on everything a party must walk) and puts the rest of its space off it — twelve pockets against thirty places on or beside the road, forty-two in all, so a little under three places in ten are optional.
- **Spacing.** Authored: at least one pocket branching from every region the road crosses (approach, barbican, west cliffs, ward, cathedral, undercroft, heights, keep), and a branch or an optional fight at least every 300 blocks of the road as the engine walks it (`critical-path-waypoints.json`): the Sally Tower off the Pilgrim Road, the Hall of Arms off the gate hall (129 blocks along the road), the Bastion at the shelf's corner (about 225), the Ossuary stair and the garden gate in the ward (348), the Last Warden-Knight in the crypt (591), the Founders' Yard north of the chapel (871), the Arbalest Turret's door on the rampart (about 1135), the Ringers' Stair beside the Tower Fire (1361), and the Buttery off the great hall (1849 on the engine's long way down, which does not open the Ringers' Stair and passes the Arbalest Turret, the garden gate and the Ossuary stair again). The largest gap between first meetings, 280 blocks from the crypt to the Founders' Yard, is under the bound.
- **What a pocket holds.** Cited shape: Stormveil's pickups run about three in ten gear, three in ten upgrade materials and a quarter consumables, the rest keys and lore; four of Morne's thirteen off-path sites are gear; every Burg detour pays. Authored: every pocket pays. Gear in seven (the Hall of Arms' greaves and the Gate-Sergeant's helm, the Bastion's bow, the Arbalest, the Bellfounder's mace, the Chandler's axe, the Hedge Knight's breastplate); Vesperhold's upgrade material is the enchanted book and the anvil it goes on, in one pocket (the Ossuary's Smite II) and one anvil (the Founders' Yard, beside Pellam's); food and arrows in four (the Sally Tower, the Gallows Walk, the Buttery, the Hall of Arms); lore in two (the beggars' tally, the founders' ledger); a door that loops back in one (the Ringers' Stair); and rank and file who pay tallow and come back after a rest in three (the Ossuary, the Gallows Walk, the east side).
- **Optional elites.** Cited: Stormveil holds two optional bosses clearly off its path and two more it lets a party avoid; the Burg and Parish together about six; the High Wall one boss and a knight. Authored: five in Vesperhold — the Last Warden-Knight, the Gate-Sergeant, the Chandler, the Hedge Knight and the Bellfounder, the one billed `boss` — each a one-time kill that drops the named piece it fought with, the shape every cited instance has.
- **Loops.** Cited: the Burg's side routes drop back onto the critical path; the High Wall's lift returns to the first bonfire; Stormveil's lifts tie its levels together. Authored: the Ringers' Stair loops the bell tower back to the cloister quarter; the Gallows Walk joins the Founders' Yard to the Hedge Garden behind the keep; the Spur Passage joins the chandlery yard to the garden.

Research consulted for rest spacing and the death penalty (ideas only unless marked):

- **Run-back length.** The engine's souls dossier (§1.4) gathers the only published times it found: the series' notorious run-backs take a minute and a half to five minutes — the Bed of Chaos's about a minute and a half — against a modern target near a minute, which Elden Ring's Stakes of Marika and Bloodborne's lamps at the arena doors exist to meet ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/longest-annoying-soulsborne-boss-runbacks/), [Fextralife, *Stakes of Marika*](https://eldenring.wiki.fextralife.com/Stakes+of+Marika)). Vanilla walking speed is 4.317 blocks a second (the dossier's measurement), so a minute is 259 blocks and the notorious tier starts near 390.
- **A rest in front of the boss.** Stormveil's grace nearest Godrick is the Secluded Cell, just short of his fog ([Fextralife, *Godrick the Grafted*](https://eldenring.wiki.fextralife.com/Godrick+the+Grafted)), the last of five graces inside the castle; Dark Souls III does not put a bonfire before every boss, and its players felt the gaps (the dossier cites a mod that retrofits them).
- **A shortcut to the boss instead of a rest.** The Burg's optional detour gives a key "to unlock a shortcut to the boss" (the Level Design Book, above); the High Wall's lift loops back to its first bonfire (Fextralife, above).
- **The penalty.** Souls drop where a body falls and are recoverable exactly once (the dossier, §7.4); a rest brings back every ordinary enemy but not the bosses, and in Dark Souls not the named elites either (§6.2, [Fextralife, *Bonfire*](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Bonfire)).

The band, and which parts of it are cited:

- **Ceiling.** Authored on the cited numbers: from the rest a death returns a party to, the walk to any fight on the road — to its arena door, the cell where the fight begins — is at most 260 blocks, a minute's walk, a third under the notorious tier's floor; at most one fight that returns on rest stands on that walk; and a fight billed `boss` has a rest within 130 blocks of its door (the Secluded Cell's and the lamps' practice; half a minute is the dossier's own box-garden figure).
- **Floor.** Authored: every stretch between two rests holds at least one road fight, so no rest is given before the stretch it rewards is walked; two rests closer than 100 blocks are allowed only where the second stands before a `boss`; no rest stands where a fight that returns on rest can perceive it (`DW0478`, the engine's proof).
- **Penalty.** Cited: the whole purse is left where the body falls and is lost only to a second death before it is picked up. Authored: the walk back to it is inside the ceiling above, and a purse lost for good is rebuilt by farming in about four rests' worth of side circuits, never by grinding the road.

## Decisions

- The second timeline is told as echoes: a sky cut and the people of that morning staged in the present room, because the engine has no timeline primitive and the one-area site holds no second space.
- The castle's exterior is designed block by block and must read at least as well as the Doune Castle site.
- The castle is a ruin in the present and whole in the echoes.
- In-game text ships in English with a `zh-cn` sidecar.
- `difficulty` is `normal`: the souls baseline, without zombie reinforcement muddying the tuned waves.
- `horizon` is a valley with a 64-block rim, so the castle stands in a mountain bowl; the approach and the causeway cross the piece's own valley floor.

- **Pellam's prices** are set against the tallow a player holds at his stall. ## Posture note

Three axes pushed off the default for this campaign: **time order** — the decisive event is shown out of order, in the echoes, after its consequences; **morality** — the final boss is right and the sympathetic keeper caused the ruin; **emotion rendering** — Warden Hesk names her fear outright ("I am afraid of the day I will not know her"). ## The castle piece

The whole site is one grammar-built piece, `prefab/vesperhold`, expanded at **172 x 104 x 292, seed 1** and bound in the campaign's one area under a `valley` horizon. `design/programs/build_castle.py` paints the site into a voxel grid from one module per part (`castle/terrain.py`, `approach.py`, `walls.py`, `ward.py`, `cathedral.py`, `undercroft.py`, `keep.py`, `belltower.py`, `gardens.py`), reading every position from `castle/layout.py`, derives every stair's shape the way vanilla does, and emits the grammar's partition as `vesperhold.json`. `finish.py` expands it into the prefab library and writes the six gate regions (`gates.json`) into the manifest — the grammar declares point anchors only — then measures the planes and the lighting and audits the piece. Re-run both to regenerate.

Heights in piece coordinates: valley floor feet 8, castle floor 24, keep dais 28, wall walks 36, bell deck 52, undercroft 12.

What the machine says about the piece at admission: every expansion gate passes (blocks exist, shapes and states complete, oriented fills, stair shapes as vanilla derives them, fluid contained); `prefab audit` passes; `walk_y` 8, `waterline_y` 11; lighting profile `dark`: 4829 of 43198 floor cells reachable on foot are below light 3 under a clear night sky (1921 at 0, 1122 at 1, 1786 at 2); the area's torch pass (`min_light` 3) lights the rest at build. The piece marks 137 point anchors — 122 cells a body stands in, thirteen furniture cells (the armory's three chests, the rampart's plate, seven pocket chests and two lecterns) and two cells nobody stands in (the bell and the bottom of the well) — and seven gate regions. The generator refuses two anchors in one cell.

The valley field is ringed by a two-course dry-stone wall on the piece's edge, and `world.boundary` holds the party four blocks beyond it, so the party's world ends where the piece does. The causeway's parapets are unbroken, iron bars between merlons, so nothing on the bridge is a way off it.
