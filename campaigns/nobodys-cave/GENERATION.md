## Notable decisions

- **Two areas, and the compiler's inter-area teleport *is* the boulder.** Areas are
  placed 256 blocks apart in the void with no walkable connection; the compiler
  emits a one-way `teleport` whenever consecutive critical-path objectives change
  area (`plan.rs:809-821`, `emit.rs:938-944`). `area/shore` (a single
  `prefab/hello-room`) holds beats 1 and 5's epilogue; `area/cave`
  (`pool/stone-keep`, 9–12 pieces) holds beats 2–4. The point of no return is
  therefore physically enforced rather than merely asserted — there is no path back
  across the void. The return trip at beat 5 is the same mechanism in reverse.
- **Polyphemus is a `minecraft:warden` NPC.** `base_entity` is a free-form string
  with no registry validation, and every NPC is summoned
  `NoAI:1b, Invulnerable:1b, Silent:1b, NoGravity:1b` plus an `interaction` hitbox
  (`emit.rs:577-596`). A Warden NPC is therefore an inert 2.9-block statue that
  talks — which solves the "villager-sized Cyclops" problem outright, and is a far
  cleaner realisation of "a blinded giant you must slip past" than a hostile wave
  would have been. The emitted `VillagerData` tag is inert on a non-villager.
  *Caveat*: `Silent:1b` also suppresses the Warden's ambient heartbeat.
- **The blinding is a `talk-to`, not an `interact`.** This keeps the strike at the
  exact spot where the NPC stands (a static NPC cannot move between the place it
  talks and the place it sleeps), avoids stacking two `interaction` hitboxes on one
  anchor, and — critically — gives the *aftermath* somewhere to live: the
  "NOBODY is killing me!" exchange with the other Cyclopes and Polyphemus's closing
  line are a dialogue node (`dlg/strike`). Without this trick the most famous beat
  in the source would have had nowhere to go (see gap 5).
- **Anchor allocation is driven by piece-uniqueness, not by taste.** `anchor/npc-stand`
  is declared by all three `keep-room-small-*` pieces and additionally collides with
  `anchor/chest` / `anchor/wave` / `anchor/door` at an identical local position in
  each; using it raised `DW0305`. Final: Eurylochus → `anchor/keeper-stand` (shore),
  Perimedes → `anchor/exit` (cave spawn hall), Polyphemus → `anchor/objective`,
  collect → `anchor/chest`, sharpen → `anchor/door`, harden → `anchor/boss`,
  ram → `anchor/wave`, boulder → `anchor/gate`.
- **Seed 1184** (the traditional date of the fall of Troy). It solved on the first
  attempt, including `keep-boss-hall` — and `anchor/objective` resolved *into*
  the boss hall, putting Polyphemus at `[321,65,-3]` and his hearth two blocks away
  at `[321,65,-1]`. The hint text was rewritten afterwards to match the solved
  geometry rather than the geometry that had been assumed.
- **The wine ships in the kit, not as a pickup.** The brief asked for "an empty
  wineskin slot", but `give-item` carries no display name, so an acquired wineskin
  would read as a plain Honey Bottle. Canon supports the substitution — Odysseus
  brings Maron's wine ashore deliberately. The wine is a pure narrative prop; the
  offering is gated by the dialogue tree, not by an item check.
- **Antiphus is never instantiated** (see gap 3). He is canonically an Ithacan the
  Cyclops devours (*Odyssey* 2.19-20), and he is named and mourned by Perimedes,
  but placing him would leave a corpse standing at his anchor for the rest of the
  delve.
- **Eurylochus stays with the ship.** Forced by the two-area split, and canon-shaped
  (Odysseus took twelve men in and left the rest at the ships). It also means the
  man who argued for leaving is the one waiting when you come out.
- **One class, not zero.** The schema requires 1–4; a single class expresses
  "no class selection", and naming it *Odysseus of Ithaca* turns the selection
  screen into the player's identity briefing.
- **In-game exposition was an explicit owner requirement.** Players unfamiliar with
  the source get three optional background branches from Eurylochus (who we are /
  where this is / what is inside), Polyphemus states his own parentage and law, and
  the class blurb names the player character. Nothing mandatory-to-read.

### 1. Wave mobs cannot be tuned (`WaveMob`)

**Wanted.** A `minecraft:warden` as the blinded Polyphemus, with reduced damage so a
20-minute demo stays survivable.

**Why not.** `WaveMob` is `{ entity, count, name? }` with `deny_unknown_fields`; any
further key is `DW0100`. Emission is a fixed template (`emit.rs:1067-1076`):
`summon <entity> <x> <y> <z> {Tags:[…],PersistenceRequired:1b,CustomName?,<default_equipment>?}`.
There is no attribute, health, or effect hook, and hand-editing compiler output is a
forbidden zone. Note the asymmetry: stage-3 `KitItem` at least *reserves*
`lore`/`enchantments`/`attributes` for M2/M3; wave mobs have no such reservation.

**Proposed.** `WaveMob.attributes: { max_health?, attack_damage?, movement_speed?,
follow_range? }`, emitted as the 1.21.11 `attribute_modifiers` component. Optionally
`WaveMob.effects` for permanent status effects.

**Impact here.** Combined with gap 6, this is why the sound-hunt encounter became a
static NPC instead of a live threat.

### 2. Dialogue cannot set flags, and options cannot be gated — **highest value**

**Wanted.** (a) The false name chosen at beat 3 changes beat 5. (b) The ending branch
and the blinding option stay hidden until earned.

**Why not.** `DialogueEffect` has exactly one variant, `CompleteObjective`
(`stages.rs:239-245`), so a conversation can never write campaign state. And
`DialogueOption` has no `requires_flags`, while `Objective` does — so every branch of
every tree is visible and readable from the very first conversation.

**Consequences suffered.** Beat 3's name choice is narratively real and mechanically
inert. Both endings and the entire blinding aftermath are reachable text from the
opening conversation; the completion effects are correctly no-ops (guarded by
`pending_guard`, `emit.rs:1278`), but **the prose leaks**. Mitigated only by
labelling those options as stage directions — `(Later, back on the sand: …)`,
`(The stake is black and hard, and he is asleep. Do it.)` — which is a convention,
not a mechanism.

**Proposed.** `DialogueEffect::SetFlag { flag }` plus `DialogueOption.requires_flags:
Vec<FlagId>`, mirroring the objective gate exactly. This single change closes gaps 2
and 4 and removes the spoiler leak. It is the most valuable authoring change on this
list.

### 3. NPCs have no lifecycle

**Wanted.** Antiphus devoured on screen at beat 2. Polyphemus talking at the mouth
and sleeping by the hearth.

**Why not.** NPCs are summoned once in `setup_finish` and never touched again. There
is no despawn, kill, move, or re-pose effect, and no way to swap a dialogue tree.

**Proposed.** `QuestEffect::DespawnNpc { npc }` and `QuestEffect::MoveNpc { npc, anchor }`.

### 4. Endings cannot branch

**Wanted.** Shouting your true name and sailing silent as two genuinely different
endings, per the source.

**Why not.** `DW0133` rejects optional quests, `DW0132` requires the finale to be the
convergent sink of the plan, and `campaign-complete` is a single terminal effect with
no payload. Two mutually exclusive terminal quests would deadlock.

**Delivered instead.** Two dialogue options that complete the *same* objective and
lead to substantially different epilogue prose (`dlg/silent`, `dlg/shout` — the
latter carries the boulder throw and Poseidon's curse). The choice is real to the
reader and invisible to the machine.

**Proposed.** `campaign-complete { ending: EndingId }` with per-ending advancement
and credits, plus a relaxation of `DW0132` to permit multiple terminal sinks when
each fires `campaign-complete`.

### 5. No scripted narration verb

**Wanted.** The Cyclopes calling in at the stone and Polyphemus roaring "NOBODY is
killing me!" — the moment the entire trick pays off.

**Why not.** Player-facing text can only hang off an NPC dialogue node or an
objective `title`/`hint`. A story beat with no NPC in the room has nowhere to live.

**Delivered instead.** Restructured the blinding into a `talk-to` on the Warden NPC
purely so the aftermath had a node to live in. This worked, but only because an NPC
happened to be present; the boulder-throw at beat 5 (no NPC in the cave any more)
could not be staged the same way and survives only as reported speech.

**Proposed.** `QuestEffect::Narrate { text, style? }` emitting chat / title /
subtitle plus a sound cue, translatable through the existing l10n inventory.

### 6. No verbs for player state or environment response

**Wanted.** Sound-based stealth (sneaking safe, sprinting and jumping draw attacks);
"the boulder answers if you try to break it".

**Why not.** No objective or condition reads player movement state, and there is no
hook for a block-break attempt. `open-gate` is strictly one-way with no `close-gate`
and no block-placement effect anywhere in the emitter.

**Proposed.** `Objective::Survive { wave, seconds }` as the minimum viable "endure a
threat" verb, and an objective-level `condition` able to read sneak/sprint state.

### 7. The harness cannot validate a lethal delve — **blocking for combat design**

Not a DSL gap; a `harness/` gap, and the direct reason the Warden could not stay
hostile.

- The bot never sneaks: zero occurrences of `sneak` / `setControlState` in
  `harness/src`, and `Movements.allowSprinting` is left at the mineflayer-pathfinder
  default (`true`) — it is maximally loud, exactly the vibration profile a Warden
  hunts.
- No `bot.on('death')`, no health check, no eating, no retry at any layer
  (sequencer, `run.ts`, compose, CI).
- Worst failure mode is multi-area-specific: on death mineflayer respawns at world
  spawn, which `setworldspawn` takes from the **first** area (`emit.rs:1206-1217`) —
  the beach. The bot then tries to pathfind to a cave anchor 256 blocks away across
  void, burns two 60-second attempts, and hard-fails with a misleading timeout.
- `difficulty` flips `peaceful → easy` automatically whenever any wave exists
  (`emit.rs:1839-1878`), so "make it peaceful" is not available to a campaign that
  has mobs at all.

**Proposed.** Sneak-aware movement with an author-visible switch; a `death` handler
that fails fast with a clear diagnostic instead of a pathfinder timeout; re-running
`select-class` after respawn.

### 8. Harness race at a cross-area teleport (**found and worked around here**)

The compiler's inter-area `teleport` is emitted into the objective-completion
function, but the harness has no knowledge that a step can relocate the player.

- `reach` executes as `walkTo(...)` and returns **immediately**, with no settle
  (`executor.ts:256-258`).
- `talk-to` executes as `walkTo(...)`, `bot.chat(command)`, then `await delay(2_000)`
  (`executor.ts:239-247`).

If the objective that triggers a cross-area teleport is a `reach`, the harness
advances to the next step and begins pathfinding **while the teleport is still in
flight** — from the old area, toward an anchor 256 blocks away across void. The path
is then invalidated mid-computation:

```
FAILED: step 9 (talk-to) failed: failed npc npc/eurylochus at [5, 65, 4] (range 3);
bot at [5.4, 65.0, 2.5]: Path was stopped before it could be completed!
```

The same delve's *other* transition (shore → cave) worked, because it happened to
fire after a `talk-to` — whose 2-second settle incidentally covers the teleport.
So the bug is invisible unless a `reach` sits immediately before an area change.

**Worked around in the DSL:** an extra `talk-to` (`obj/hold-fast`, Perimedes at the
cave mouth) was inserted after the ram objective, so the transition once again fires
behind a settle. The story wanted that beat anyway — it is where the ram trick gets
explained — but the campaign should not have had to know this.

**Proper fix (harness).** Make the executor teleport-aware: after any step, if the
player's position jumps discontinuously, re-read position and restart the next
`walkTo` rather than failing; or add a settle to `reach`; or have `critical-path.json`
mark steps that carry a transport so the harness can wait for it deliberately. The
last option is best — the compiler already knows exactly which objectives teleport
(`plan.transport`), and that knowledge is currently thrown away before it reaches
the harness.

### 9. The bot never touches entities — a validation blind spot

`talk-to` and `interact` are executed as `/trigger` chat commands, not as clicks
(`executor.ts:239-247`, `386-393`). A **failed NPC summon would therefore not be
caught by the bot ladder** — the critical path would still pass with no NPC in the
world. This matters most for exactly the kind of experiment this campaign performs
(a non-villager `base_entity`).

**Proposed.** A PackTest assertion that every `dw_npc_<id>` tag resolves to exactly
one entity after `setup_finish`.

### 10. Compiler bug — a terminal dialogue node crashes the server (**found and worked around here**)

**Severity: server will not boot.** Not a design gap; a live emission bug, and this
campaign is the first to trigger it.

`DialogueNode.options` is documented in the schema as *"Branching options; empty
closes the dialog"* — so `"options": []` is legal DSL and `delvec validate` /
`analyze` accept it with zero diagnostics. But the emitter maps **every** node to
`"type": "minecraft:multi_action"`, and a terminal node therefore ships as:

```json
{ "type": "minecraft:multi_action", "actions": [], "after_action": "close", … }
```

1.21.11's `multi_action` codec requires a non-empty list, so registry loading aborts
at boot:

```
Errors in registry minecraft:dialog:
>> Errors in element nobodys-cave:eurylochus_silent:
   Failed to parse … Caused by: java.lang.IllegalStateException: List must have contents
Failed to load datapacks, can't proceed with server load.
```

Both ladder profiles died here before executing a single test or bot step — PackTest
exited 255, the bot container never started because the server never became healthy.

**Worked around in the DSL** (compiler output must never be hand-edited): the two
ending nodes `dlg/silent` and `dlg/shout` were given a single no-op closing option
each rather than an empty list.

**Proper fix (compiler, not filed as a spec change — it is a bug).** Emit a node with
no options as `minecraft:notice`, which has an implicit single close button, instead
of `multi_action`. Add a `crates/compiler` regression test asserting no emitted dialog
carries an empty `actions` list, and ideally a `delvec` diagnostic so this is caught
at compile time rather than at server boot.

**Note on why this was missed until now:** `hollow-vigil` gives every node at least
one option, so it never produced a terminal node. Any campaign that ends a
conversation without offering a choice hits this immediately.

### 11. `give-item` cannot carry a display name

**Wanted.** The brief's "empty wineskin slot" — a wineskin acquired in the cave and
recognisable as Maron's wine.

**Why not.** `QuestEffect::GiveItem` is `{ item, count }` with no `name`, while
stage-3 `KitItem` *does* support a display name. An item handed over mid-delve
therefore cannot be named, and a quest-significant object arrives as a plain
"Honey Bottle".

**Delivered instead.** The wine ships pre-filled and named in the starting kit.
Canon supports it (Odysseus carries Maron's wine ashore deliberately), but the
choice was forced by the emitter, not by the story.

**Proposed.** Add optional `name` to `GiveItem`, matching `KitItem`.

### 12. `anchor/npc-stand` is a dead anchor (prefab metadata)

`anchor/npc-stand` is declared by all three `keep-room-small-*` pieces, so
referencing it raises `DW0305` (ambiguous resolution). **That diagnostic worked
exactly as designed and caught the mistake at build time — `DW0305` is a success
story here, not a complaint.**

The real defect is underneath it: in each of those pieces `anchor/npc-stand` sits at
a position *identical* to another anchor in the same piece — `anchor/chest` in `-a`,
`anchor/wave` in `-b`, `anchor/door` in `-c`. So even after disambiguation it would
put an NPC inside a chest, on a wave spawn point, or on top of an interact marker
and its hitbox. The anchor is unusable as declared.

**Proposed.** Give `anchor/npc-stand` a distinct position in each piece, or drop it.

### 13. `collect` chests and `interact` markers are placed at setup, ungated — **and one can stall an objective**

**Component**: compiler / emitter · **Found by owner playtest, 2026-07-31**

Both surfaced from a single playtest note: *"这里没跟巨人对话就能拿东西？"* — the
player could loot the olive beam out of the pens before ever meeting Polyphemus.

`setup_finish` places **every** `collect` chest (`setblock … chest` +
`item replace … container.0`) and summons **every** `interact` hitbox and its glowing
`item_display` marker, for all objectives, at world setup. None of it is gated on the
objective being active. So the whole puzzle chain is physically present, lootable and
readable from minute one — a marker named "Grind the Point" glows beside the entrance
before the player has any idea what a point is, and the beam can be pocketed three
beats early.

**The worse half is a latent stall.** A `collect` objective completes via an
`inventory_changed` advancement whose reward function re-checks the guards and then
revokes itself to re-arm:

```
execute if score @s dw.qa_the_stake matches 1 if score @s dw.f_named matches 1
        unless score @s dw.o_olive_beam matches 1 run function …:complete_o_olive_beam
advancement revoke @s only nobodys-cave:c_olive_beam
```

If the item is taken while the guards are unsatisfied, the advancement fires, does
nothing, and re-arms — but `inventory_changed` will not fire again merely because the
player is *still holding* the item. Once the objective does activate, it stays
incomplete until some unrelated inventory change happens to re-trigger it. In a delve
with little else to pick up, a player can be left holding the quest item with the
objective stuck open and no indication why. **This affects every `collect` objective
in every campaign, not just this one.**

**Fix.** Gate placement on activation: place the chest / summon the marker when the
objective activates rather than at `setup_finish` (the tick loop already computes
activation for the announce). Failing that, re-check `collect` guards on a tick for
players already holding the item, so a re-armed advancement is not the only path to
completion.

**Mitigated here, not fixed**: see the guidance change below — the campaign now keeps
an explicit active instruction on screen at all times so the player is steered to
Polyphemus first. That reduces the odds of tripping it; it does not close it.

### 14. Interaction props are hardcoded, and objectives cannot target entities — **why nothing looks like what it is**

**Component**: DSL + emitter · **Raised by owner at playtest, 2026-07-31**:
*"藏到羊肚子下面为什么不用羊，磨尖为什么不用砂轮，烤硬为什么不用篝火？"*

Fair question, and the answer is the same for all three. The marker entity is a
literal in the emitter:

```rust
// interact
… item_display … item:{id:"minecraft:lantern",count:1}   // emit.rs
// reach-anchor
… item_display … item:{id:"minecraft:end_rod",count:1}
```

So a grindstone, a hearth fire and a ram all render as **two floating lanterns and an
end rod**. There is no author-facing field for the prop, and no way to put a real
block at an anchor: the emitter writes exactly three kinds of block anywhere in its
output — solver socket seals (`fill`, `emit.rs:543`), the `collect` chest
(`setblock … minecraft:chest`, `emit.rs:613`), and the `open-gate` air fill
(`emit.rs:1178`). Nothing else. A campfire cannot be placed by any means available to
a campaign.

Separately, **objectives address anchors, never entities.** `interact` summons its own
`minecraft:interaction` hitbox at a fixed position, so "get under a ram" cannot refer
to the rams — even though `wave/the-flock` puts six live sheep on that exact anchor.
The sheep are decoration standing next to the thing the player actually activates.

**The precedent for the fix already exists.** `collect` places a *real chest* and uses
it as its own affordance — no hologram needed, and it reads correctly in game. Doing
the same for `interact` is a small, symmetrical change:

- `Objective::Interact.prop: { block: "minecraft:grindstone" }` — `setblock` the prop
  at the anchor and let it be the visual, exactly as `collect` does with its chest.
  Falls back to the current lantern when omitted, so existing campaigns are unchanged.
- `QuestEffect::SetBlock { anchor, block }` as the general form (also closes the
  "boulder that answers when struck" half of gap 6, and gives `open-gate` a partner).
- `Objective::InteractEntity { wave, … }` for fictions that are inherently about a
  creature — riding, hiding under, leading, calming. Nothing in v0.3 can express any
  of them.

**Impact.** Together with the single-tileset problem in *Visual review*, this is the
larger half of why the delve reads correctly and does not yet *look* correct. The
writing carries the Odyssey; the props are three glowing hologram icons in a castle.
Of everything on this list, gap 14 plus a cave tileset would do the most for how the
demo actually feels, and neither requires new gameplay semantics — only the ability
to say what a thing looks like.

1. *"这里是入口附近，对完话缺乏引导… 磨出尖来应该是后面才会用到的，不应该放在这里或者应该在到那一步之前隐藏"* —
   after the Perimedes conversation the player had **no active objective text at all**,
   while a marker for a much later step glowed next to them.
2. *"这里没跟巨人对话就能拿东西？引导做的不够好"* — see gap 13.

**Root cause of both, on the campaign's side:** no `talk-to` objective carried a
`title`/`hint`. The skill guidance says `talk-to` *may* omit them because "the NPC
dialog is self-explanatory" — that holds when the NPC is in front of you, and fails
completely when the next NPC is 60 blocks away through an unfamiliar cave. The
player was left with a silent objective and an unrelated glowing marker, and did the
reasonable thing: wandered off and looted the pens.

**Fixed in the DSL**: all six `talk-to` objectives now carry a `title` and a
landmark-relative `hint`. The one for `obj/the-name` also does sequencing work the
DSL cannot do structurally — *"Go and talk to him before you help yourself to
anything of his."*

**Lesson for the skill**: treat `title`/`hint` on `talk-to` as required whenever the
target NPC is not already visible from where the previous objective completed. The
"may omit" allowance should be read narrowly.

## Things that worked, and are worth keeping

Recorded because they are load-bearing techniques a future campaign should reuse
rather than rediscover.

- **`base_entity` accepts any entity id, and NPCs are inert by construction.** Every
  NPC is summoned `NoAI:1b, Invulnerable:1b, Silent:1b, NoGravity:1b, PersistenceRequired:1b`
  plus a separate `interaction` hitbox (`emit.rs:577-596`), and there is no registry
  validation on the field. A `minecraft:warden` NPC is therefore a 2.9-block statue
  that talks and cannot hurt anyone — the live server accepted the summon with no
  error, warning, or `Invalid`/`Unable to summon` line anywhere in the log. This is
  the single most useful discovery of the run and deserves to be documented as a
  supported technique rather than left as folklore. Caveats: `Silent:1b` also
  suppresses the Warden's ambient heartbeat, and the emitted `VillagerData` tag is
  inert on a non-villager.
- **Multi-area plus auto-transport is narratively powerful, not just a layout tool.**
  A one-way teleport with no walkable path back is an *enforced* point of no return
  rather than an asserted one. See gap 8 for its one sharp edge.
- **Determinism held across every iteration** — byte-identical rebuild each time,
  including after the mid-run restructures.
- **`DW0305` and `DW0306` both earned their keep**, catching an ambiguous anchor and
  proving gate-aware reachability on a campaign whose gate seals content behind it.

## Visual review (spec-0003 visual tier)

`delve-render fidelity-gate` passed; the 14-prefab / 111-shot batch rendered clean.
Checked against each shot's `expect`: rooms assembled, walls/floor/ceiling intact,
no floating or clipped blocks at piece bounds, no dark frames — every declared
`lit` piece reads as lit.

**The one real finding is aesthetic, and it is a library gap, not a build defect:
every piece is the same grey stone-brick keep.** The prose of this campaign is a
bronze-age cave on a Mediterranean island; the architecture the player actually
walks through is a castle interior, and `prefab/hello-room` — standing in for the
landing beach — renders as a featureless grey cube. Nothing in the current library
reads as rock, sand, water, sky or firelight.

Consequences accepted for this demo, and worth a prefab commission before it is
shown to anyone as a showcase:

- No storybook media ships with this campaign. Under spec-0007 the storybook takes
  exterior/starting-scene images from the render set, but the available exterior is
  a grey box that actively misrepresents the delve. Shipping nothing is the more
  honest choice; hand-crafted shots or a cave tileset can fill `media/` later.
- **A cave/shore tileset is the highest-value asset work this campaign implies**:
  a rock-walled pool with a sealed mouth, pens, a hearth, and one open-air beach
  piece. That single addition would move this delve from "reads right" to "looks
  right" without touching a line of DSL.

Note the renders cover **prefabs only** — NPCs, chests and markers are runtime
summons and do not appear. Verification that the Warden NPC actually materialises
had to come from the live server run, not from here (and see gap 8: the bot alone
would not have caught a failed summon).
